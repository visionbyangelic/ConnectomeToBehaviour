## 1. The question this rung answers

What does the frozen whole-brain model do, brain-only, under each of the four gustatory drive conditions — sugar, bitter, sugar+bitter, water — measured as per-neuron firing rates with confidence intervals, and in particular: **does simultaneous bitter drive suppress the MN9 response to sugar drive, and by how much?** That suppression measurement is the project's RQ-C2 brain-only measurement, and the four conditions together are the baseline Part 7's lesion results are read against.

**Language discipline (binding on every report of this rung).** This rung has **no external reference output** for three of its four conditions: no released simulation or dataset records what this model "should" do under bitter, sugar+bitter, or water drive. The rung is therefore a **measurement of our frozen model's own responses** — model behaviour under registered drive conventions — not a reproduction, not a benchmark, and not biological validation. Only the sugar condition touches validated ground, and only indirectly: through the anchor check (§8.1) against the `sugarR` rung's realised values, which themselves passed against Shiu et al.'s simulation output. Nothing in this rung is evidence about a living fly, animal electrophysiology, or animal behaviour, and no report may word it that way.

**The amendment's additions, in one paragraph.** The sensitivity-arms amendment (24 Nov 2026) adds two measurement sets under the panel's LOCKED/ARMED ruling: **(i)** four bitter dose-response arms — bitter alone at 100 Hz and 150 Hz, and sugar+bitter with the bitter drive at 100 Hz and 150 Hz (sugar fixed at 200 Hz) — which read how the suppression measurement moves with the one drive convention this rung could not justify independently (§3, §8.5); and **(ii)** one bilateral secondary condition (v) — both sugar sets driven together — which measures the model's response to bilateral input descriptively. Neither set carries a confirmatory claim; their governing rules are §8.5, their design is §4, and their statistics are §7.2.

## 2. The validated envelope, stated before the design

Part 3 certified exactly one stimulus configuration (`PART4-SENSORY-SPECS.md` §3): **the right sugar GRN set (21 neurons) driven at 200 Hz**, brain-only, against Shiu et al.'s simulation output. That is the whole of the validated envelope. Bitter (21), water (18), any combination such as sugar+bitter, and bilateral sugar drive (the amendment's secondary condition (v), §3) are **OUTSIDE THE VALIDATED ENVELOPE**, and every result of this rung that uses them is labelled that way wherever it appears: the brain's response to those drives is this project's extrapolation from a validated model, not a validated behaviour of it. This rung does not extend the envelope — measuring a response is not validating it — and no Part 5 or Part 7 document may cite this rung as having validated the bitter, water, or combined drives.

**Why 30 trials.** The rung's comparisons are against the sugar condition and against the `sugarR` rung's values, both of which rest on 30 trials; matching that N keeps the sampling precision comparable across every comparison this rung makes, and 30 exceeds Part 0's confirmatory floor (N = 20 seeds). **Seed semantics.** As `PART3-PREREG.md` §4: each seed initialises all stochastic components of its trial — in this model the only stochastic component is the Poisson drive — the runner derives trial seeds as base seed + trial index, per-trial streams are independent, and a trial's output is reproducible from its own seed alone. **Wall-clock — measured basis, projected totals (confirmation round, Reviewer 1).** The per-condition figure is **measured, not estimated**: the Part 3 primary run — same code path, same full network, 30 trials × 1.0 s — took **2,094.6 s ≈ 35 min** of wall clock for its 30 trials (`PART3-RESULTS.md` §1), and ≈ 35 min per 30-trial condition is the measured rate every figure in this paragraph is built on. Everything beyond that one run is a **projection** at that measured rate: four primary conditions project to **≈ 2.3 h**; **as amended,** the four bitter arms are four further 30-trial conditions, adding **≈ 2.3 h**, and the bilateral condition adds **≈ 0.6 h** — a projected total for the rung's nine conditions of **≈ 5.2 h (≈ 4.6–5.7 h across the plausible range**; the VM is shared and contention stretches wall-clock, not correctness). The determinism check adds two single trials (minutes). This is nowhere near the GPU trigger (14 days / 72 h per family, Part 0 §4) — the CPU reference platform stands, and K4 is not implicated.

**Run integrity.** The runner refuses a non-empty run directory, so a crashed or partial attempt voids the run: the attempt is logged, and the re-run takes a new `run_id`. No partial trial sets are ever analysed.

## 6. Firing-rate computation

Identical to `PART3-PREREG.md` §6, so this rung's numbers are commensurable with rung 3's:

- Per neuron, per trial: spike count with 0 ≤ t < 1.0 s, divided by 1.0 s → rate in Hz. (Every trial is exactly 1.0 s in every condition.)
- Per neuron: the **mean of the 30 per-trial rates**, per condition.
- **No burn-in, no transient trimming** — the onset transient from the resting start is part of every output, on the same terms as rung 3.
- **Inactive neurons are explicit zeros.** Rate tables are built over all 127,400 v630 IDs; a neuron absent from a condition's spike table has rate 0 in every trial of that condition.

## 7. Comparison sets and statistics

**Active sets.** A neuron is *active* in a condition if and only if it emits **at least one spike across that condition's full 30-trial window** — equivalently, mean rate **> 0 Hz** (one spike in 30 s ≈ 0.033 Hz), the `PART3-PREREG.md` §7 definition, unchanged. Per condition, the active count is reported at the primary definition **and** at the two further thresholds rung 3 pre-declared, **0.1 Hz** and **1 Hz** mean rate, so the ladder's set-size reporting stays comparable across rungs (rung 3's reference counts: 448 / 434 / 391; rung 3's own output: 446 active at the primary definition).

**The union set U2.** The union of the four conditions' active sets. Per-neuron mean rates are tabulated over U2 for **all four conditions** (a neuron inactive in a condition enters with rate 0 there), so every cross-condition comparison is computed over one fixed set, never varied per comparison. Neurons silent in all four conditions are counted and reported, and enter no comparison.

**MN9 endpoints.** MN9 left (720575940660219265) and right (720575940645521262), reported **separately, always**: per condition, per-trial rates, mean, and **95% CI by percentile bootstrap over trials, 10,000 resamples**, computed identically for every condition. Fixed bootstrap seed **20261127**, declared here in advance — **PROPOSED** *(reasoning: Part 3's discipline is one declared-in-advance seed per bootstrap purpose; 20261127 continues the 202611xx sequence without reusing 20261123 (splits), 20261124 (Part 3 MN9), or the analysis seeds declared in `PART3-RESULTS.md` (20261125, 20261126))*. The same seed serves the Δ confidence intervals of §8.2, as Part 3's single MN9 seed served both its CIs and its Δ test.

**Driven-population readout (manipulation check — reported, not a criterion).** Under this model's drive a driven neuron fires at close to the Poisson rate by construction (`PART3-PREREG.md` §3/A1), so each condition's driven set's own mean firing rate reads out the drive actually delivered. Per condition, the mean rate of each driven population is reported (sugar+bitter reports both), alongside the mean rates of the non-driven GRN populations. A driven set firing far from 200 Hz is a diagnosis-path fact (§9), not a verdict input: no claim in §8 is decided by it, and it may not be promoted into a criterion after the fact.

**The headline comparison — the suppression ratio.** Per trial, the **pooled MN9 rate** is (MN9 L spikes + MN9 R spikes) / 1.0 s — the sum of the two per-side rates. (The ratio below is invariant to the sum-vs-mean choice; the sum is used so the pooled figure is the same quantity Part 5's chain calls the pooled rate.) The **suppression ratio** is:

> (mean pooled MN9 rate in sugar+bitter) ÷ (mean pooled MN9 rate in sugar),

with a **95% CI by percentile bootstrap over trials within each condition, 10,000 resamples** — fixed bootstrap seed **20261128**, declared here in advance — **PROPOSED** *(reasoning: a separate declared seed for the ratio, so the headline's resampling stream is independent of the MN9-CI stream and auditable on its own)*. The ratio is the **ratio of condition means** (mean pooled MN9 rate in sugar+bitter ÷ mean pooled MN9 rate in sugar); its CI comes from resampling trials **independently within each condition** — each resample draws 30 trial indices with replacement from the sugar trials and, independently, 30 from the sugar+bitter trials, recomputing both means and their ratio. The four conditions use independent seed blocks (§4), so no real pairing exists between conditions: trial-index pairing is **only a bookkeeping convention** for laying the two trial lists side by side, and nothing in this rung averages per-trial ratios — the ratio of means is the estimator throughout. Per-side suppression ratios (L: sugar+bitter ÷ sugar on MN9 L; R likewise) are computed by the same procedure and **reported alongside**, descriptively: they are not the headline and not part of the §8.2 family.

**Bitter-alone and water-alone against the undriven baseline (descriptive).** With no drive at all, this model is silent — the undriven state is 0 Hz (verified at Part 2; in the `sugarR` reference file every undriven GRN population likewise fired at exactly 0.0 Hz). Bitter-alone and water-alone MN9 L/R means are reported with their §7 CIs and described against that 0 Hz baseline — **descriptively**: any nonzero MN9 response there is network-mediated by construction, and no test is registered on the baseline comparison. The registered tests on bitter and water are the §8.2 differences against the sugar condition.

### 7.1 What is and is not pre-registered per condition (Amendment Pass 1 — Reviewer 1 verbatim-round condition)

- **Bitter-alone and water-alone carry no confirmatory claim in this rung.** Their MN9 rates and active-set tables are reported **descriptively**, with CIs. The §8.2 family already covers their MN9 differences against the sugar condition as named comparisons; nothing else about them is claimed.
- **No condition in this rung is compared against a reference simulation output.** The sugar condition's anchor (§8.1) compares against our own frozen implementation's realised output (the Part 3 primary run's MN9 means), not against Shiu et al.'s file — the reference file's own values are reported alongside for context and are not the anchor. Bitter, sugar+bitter and water have no reference output at all. Their results are therefore **model behaviour under the registered conventions, not reproduction** — and the rung as a whole is a measurement of the frozen model's responses, per §1, not a benchmark.
- **"The ratio" (§8.3) defined.** The headline suppression ratio is **pooled MN9 rate (sugar+bitter) ÷ pooled MN9 rate (sugar)**, computed as the ratio of condition means as defined in §7 above. The per-side ratios (L and R) are reported alongside. **No per-side condition is required for the headline claim**: the pooled ratio is the deciding quantity; if the pooled ratio meets its rule and a per-side ratio points the other way, that split is reported as the finding it is, not averaged away (see §8.3's final paragraph, which now states this explicitly).

## 8. Decision rules

This rung is a measurement rung, not a benchmark: it has exactly one stop rule (the anchor, §8.1) and one headline claim rule (the suppression ratio, §8.3), plus a small named family of difference tests (§8.2). Everything else is reported measurement. There is no rung-level PASS/FAIL beyond the anchor, and no outcome of §8.2 or §8.3 makes the rung "fail" — each carries its own pre-stated wording, including *inconclusive*.

### 8.1 The anchor check (stop rule)

For each of L and R, let Δ = (this rung's sugar-condition mean MN9 rate) − (the `sugarR` primary run's mean MN9 rate), where the Part 3 primary values are **L 92.57 Hz, R 62.40 Hz** (`PART3-RESULTS.md` §3; the reference file's own values, L 93.27 / R 61.80 Hz, are reported alongside for context but are **not** the anchor — the anchor is against our own frozen implementation's realised output, because reproducibility of the frozen implementation is what is being checked). The 95% CI for each Δ is computed by percentile bootstrap over trials on both sides (10,000 resamples, seed 20261127, §7).

**The anchor passes if and only if:** both Δ CIs lie **entirely within the Part 0 band** (PART0-PACKAGE §2.4: ±25% of the comparison value, or ±5 Hz, whichever is wider) applied to the Part 3 primary means — numerically **±23.1 Hz for L** (25% of 92.57) and **±15.6 Hz for R** (25% of 62.40); the ±5 Hz floor is not binding for either neuron — **and** the L > R ordering of this rung's sugar point estimates is preserved. — **PROPOSED** *(reasoning: this mirrors `PART3-PREREG.md` §8 clause 4's difference/equivalence test exactly, with our own realised values in the reference role; a looser check would let a drifted implementation carry Part 7's baseline, a tighter one would re-litigate rung 3)*.

**If the anchor fails: STOP.** The rung does not complete. Any condition runs already made are quarantined as diagnostic outputs — they are not rung results and may not be reported as such — and the diagnosis path (§9) runs before anything else. The failure is a statement about the frozen implementation's reproducibility under this freeze, and it goes to the HARD gate: no Part 7 reliance may be placed on this rung, and no re-run of the anchor under new seeds is permitted to convert the failure (seeds are never re-rolled, §4).

**Non-gating report (Amendment Pass 1 — Reviewer 1 verbatim-round condition).** The anchor's Δ and its 95% CI are reported relative to zero in the results report, alongside the band check. If either side's Δ CI **excludes 0 by more than 5 Hz** (i.e. its nearer endpoint lies beyond ±5 Hz), that is reported to the panel as a **discrepancy** — an unexplained shift in the frozen implementation's output that sampling noise alone (the anchor's own CI width, of order a few Hz) does not explain. It does **not** trigger STOP and does not change the anchor's pass/fail verdict: the ±25% band remains the gate, because a STOP is costly and the band is the pre-registered gate; the discrepancy rule exists so a ~20 Hz shift inside the band is never silently passed.

### 8.2 The named family — MN9 differences against the sugar condition (Holm)

The confirmatory difference family of this rung is **six comparisons**: for each non-sugar condition (bitter, sugar+bitter, water) and each side (L, R), Δ = (that condition's mean MN9 rate) − (the sugar condition's mean MN9 rate), with a 95% CI by the §7 bootstrap (seed 20261127). — **PROPOSED** *(reasoning: the family is kept to the feeding readout this rung exists to measure — six comparisons, each one a question Part 7 will actually ask of the baseline; the whole-population rate tables of §7 are reported descriptively and carry no confirmatory claims, so they need no place in the family)*.

Each comparison's two-sided bootstrap p-value is p = 2 × min(P(Δ ≤ 0), P(Δ ≥ 0)) over the bootstrap distribution, capped at 1. **Holm** correction is applied across the six p-values (PART0-PACKAGE §2.3; FINAL-PLAN §7.4). A difference is **claimed** only where the Holm-adjusted p < 0.05; every comparison's Δ and unadjusted 95% CI are reported regardless of the claim outcome. A comparison whose CI excludes 0 but whose adjusted p does not reach 0.05 is reported as an unadjusted-CI observation, not a claimed difference — the two records are never blurred.

### 8.3 The suppression claim rule (headline)

Decided on the §7 suppression ratio and its CIs, in this order:

- **Suppression** is claimed if and only if the ratio's **95% CI lies entirely below 1**.
- **Enhancement** is claimed if and only if the 95% CI lies entirely above 1 (the rule is symmetric by construction; an enhancement would be reported in those words, not massaged toward the expected direction).
- **Equivalence** is claimed if and only if neither of the above holds **and** the ratio's **90% CI lies entirely within [0.85, 1.15]** — TOST within ±0.15 absolute (PART0-PACKAGE §2.4), each one-sided test at 0.05, the 90%-CI-within-bounds rule.
- **Otherwise the comparison is INCONCLUSIVE** — a reported result with its own wording, never a null, never "no suppression", never a quiet pass.

The per-side ratios (§7) are reported with the headline whatever its outcome; if the pooled headline and a per-side ratio point in different directions, that is reported as the finding it is, not averaged away. **No per-side condition is required for the headline claim** (see §7.1): the pooled ratio is the deciding quantity.

**Mixed headline, pre-written (Amendment Pass 2 — Reviewer 1):** if the pooled ratio's 95% CI meets the suppression (or enhancement) rule while **either** per-side ratio's 95% CI lies **entirely on the opposite side of 1** from the pooled result, the headline reads **"mixed: pooled suppression with a contrary per-side result"** (or "mixed: pooled enhancement with a contrary per-side result", as applicable), with both numbers adjacent — the pooled headline and the contrary per-side figure — and the split is reported as the finding it is. This rule is fixed before results exist so no post-hoc headline wording is invented at report time.

### 8.4 What completion means

The rung **completes** when: the determinism check has passed; all four conditions have run under the freeze with clean manifests (§5); the anchor has passed (§8.1); and every comparison of §§8.2–8.3 has been reported under its pre-stated rule, with the §7 tables complete. The suppression comparison then carries exactly one of its four outcomes (suppression / enhancement / equivalence / inconclusive), each of which is a legitimate, reportable result of the rung. Completion is what Part 7's prerequisite requires — not any particular suppression outcome.

### 8.5 Sensitivity arms and the bilateral secondary condition — governing rules (sensitivity-arms amendment, 24 Nov 2026)

Added before the freeze, on the panel's unanimous Item 2 ruling (LOCKED / ARMED) and Angelic's approval, at a moment when **no rung-2 trial of any kind has run** — the arms are pre-registered in the strict sense, not retrofitted to any result. The ruling's rules bind every arm in this rung:

- **One locked primary governs.** The four primary conditions, the anchor (§8.1), the §8.2 family and the §8.3 headline are the rung's confirmatory content, unchanged. An arm can contextualise or bound a primary finding; it can never **rescue, replace, redefine, or be pooled with** a primary result. A failed or inconclusive primary stays failed or inconclusive whatever the arms show.
- **Non-confirmatory, labelled.** The bitter arms are **[PRE-REGISTERED SENSITIVITY ARM — NON-CONFIRMATORY]** in every table, figure and report. They sit outside the §8.2 Holm family and outside every TOST family; their CIs are unadjusted (§7.2); the words "confirmed" and "significant" are never used of them. Condition (v) is labelled a **separate secondary stimulus condition, OUTSIDE THE VALIDATED ENVELOPE** (§8.5, final bullet).
- **Disjoint, pre-allocated seeds.** Arms use only their §4 blocks (6230–6379); no primary seed is reused, and no arm seed enters any primary computation.
- **No arm after a result.** These arms exist because they were registered before any rung result was seen. No further arm may be added to this rung after any primary result exists, except by formal amendment approved before that arm executes — and an arm added then is labelled exploratory, never pre-registered.

**The bitter arms' reading — dose-response, stated in advance.** The 200 Hz convention for bitter is a project convention, "matched to sugar, not independently justified" (§3). The arms exist to measure what that convention costs: the suppression ratio depends on the bitter drive rate by mechanism (a stronger bitter drive is a stronger suppressive input), so the arms are read as a **dose-response** — the suppression ratio and the MN9 rates tabulated across bitter drive rates **{100, 150, 200} Hz** (200 = the primary conditions), sugar fixed at 200 Hz throughout. The claim, whatever the numbers, is **scoped to the registered rates**: "under the registered convention of 200 Hz, [the §8.3 outcome]; across the registered dose-response the ratio moved [as measured]." The arms do **not** establish which rate is biologically correct — no reference exists to establish it against — and they do not validate the encoder; the Part 4 §10 drive-rate sensitivity obligation is neither discharged nor weakened by them (§3). If the §8.3 outcome would differ at another registered rate, that dependence is reported plainly as the finding; the favourable rate is never selected and presented as the result.

**The four arms, named in full, and why these two rates (confirmation round — Reviewer 1's justification rule; Reviewer 2's wording note).** The four arms are: bitter @ 100 Hz (seeds 6230–6259); bitter @ 150 Hz (seeds 6260–6289); sugar+bitter @ bitter 100 Hz, sugar fixed at 200 Hz (seeds 6290–6319); and **sugar+bitter @ 150 Hz** — sugar+bitter with the bitter drive at 150 Hz and sugar fixed at 200 Hz (seeds 6320–6349). Each alternative is justified in writing, as the panel's rule requires. **150 Hz** is the upstream model's own default drive rate — the default in the released code, and the other candidate generating rate this project adjudicated in Part 3, where the reference data proved to have been generated at 200 Hz rather than at the code's 150 Hz default — so it is the most decision-relevant alternative: it is the rate the convention would have carried had the code's default been trusted. **100 Hz** is justified differently, and stated honestly: it is a **design choice**, not a sourced value — the evenly spaced lower rung that gives the dose ladder {100, 150, 200} Hz its uniform spacing. No literature source is claimed for 100 Hz.

**Water rate arms — considered and NOT run (decision on record).** The panel's ruling armed the bitter convention; Reviewer 1's proposal skipped water arms unless their cost was trivial. They are **not registered**: the water condition carries no confirmatory claim in this rung (§7.1), its §8.2 differences against sugar are computed at the primary convention, and no decision anywhere in the project turns on water's rate sensitivity — so the omission is a decision, recorded here, not a gap. A water dose-response, if ever wanted, arrives by amendment on its own justification.

**Condition (v) — bilateral sugar, a separate secondary condition.** Bilateral drive recruits a different population from condition (i) (both sugar sets, 31 neurons, against the right set's 21), so it is registered as its own condition, not as a sensitivity setting of the sugar condition. Its registered purpose is narrow and descriptive: **whether the MN9 L/R asymmetry and the pooled response change in size when both sugar sets are driven together** — the asymmetry being RQ-C5's open question (§10), on which this condition may report measurements but, its sets being at their natural unequal counts (10 left + 21 right), **no resolution**: rung 6's matched-count design owns resolution. Condition (v) is **never pooled** with condition (i), is not part of the §8.2 family, carries no claim rule, and stands in no relation to the anchor: the anchor (§8.1) neither depends on it nor can be rescued by it, and it runs only after the anchor has passed (§4 run order). Part 5's arm (e) is this condition's embodied counterpart, registered there as the same kind of object.

**Completion, restated with the arms.** The arms and condition (v) are part of the frozen design: the rung's record is complete as amended only when they have run under the freeze and been reported under this section's rules (§11). §8.4's completion definition — the four conditions, the anchor, §§8.2–8.3 — is unchanged and remains what Part 7's prerequisite requires; the arms neither gate it nor rescue it.

## 9. On anchor failure — the diagnosis path

Per the plan's discrepancy rule: STOP, log, diagnose — in this pre-set order, each step's outcome recorded before the next begins (the `PART3-PREREG.md` §9 order, adapted to a reproducibility failure):

1. **The analysis code first.** The rung-2 analysis script is re-verified on synthetic spike tables with known rates and a known suppression ratio (including a ratio of exactly 1 and a suppressed case) — a bug in the measuring instrument is the cheapest explanation and is excluded first.
2. **Run provenance.** The sugar run's manifest: this rung's freeze commit, dirty flag, data hashes, environment — any deviation voids the run, not the implementation.
3. **The driven set.** The 21 driven IDs and their Brian indices re-checked against `id_sets_v1`.
4. **The drive.** Rate, kick size (68.75 mV into `v`), and refractory handling re-read against the reading log and the frozen code; the §7 driven-population readout is evidence here.
5. **The weights.** The sampled sign audit re-run; loader counts re-verified.
6. **The numerics.** dt, integration method, reset string re-checked against the spec.
7. **Only then**, freeze-integrity explanations (e.g. the implementation inside this rung's freeze commit is not in fact byte-identical to `0cb9b81` despite the §12 check, or an environment difference) — and only with evidence, not as a resting place.

**No parameter is re-fit during diagnosis.** `w_syn` stays at the paper's 0.275 mV and every other parameter at its reading-log value; any proposal to change a parameter is a formal amendment that voids this rung's confirmatory status and must say so. K1 is **not** re-implicated by this rung — it was rung 3's criterion and rung 3 is closed — but an anchor failure that survives diagnosis is a freeze-integrity finding, and it blocks Part 7 (whose prerequisite this rung is) until the HARD gate decides what it means.

## 10. RQ-C2 and RQ-C5 handling

**RQ-C2 (bitter suppression).** This rung is RQ-C2's brain-only measurement: whether bitter drive suppresses the sugar response *in the brain model*, read from MN9 under §8.3's rule, with no body, no decoder, and no body-side or decoder-side rule anywhere in the path — suppression, if it appears, comes from the brain's response to the bitter GRN drive or it does not appear (`PART4-SENSORY-SPECS.md` §3.2). Part 5's in-loop bitter arm is a separate, embodied measurement under its own preregistration; neither substitutes for the other, and any disagreement between them is reported as a finding, not resolved by picking the convenient one.

**RQ-C5 (the MN9 asymmetry).** The asymmetry question — whether the L > R MN9 asymmetry under right-only sugar drive is a circuit property, an artefact of the unequal GRN set sizes (21 right vs 10 left), or a bug — stays **open** through this rung (`PART3-PREREG.md` §10). This rung's sugar condition adds a second seeded 30-trial measurement of the asymmetry's size, reported with CIs, L and R always separate. What it may support: whether the asymmetry *appears* in each condition and how large it is, per condition. What it may not support: any claim about *why*, in any condition. The suppression ratio's pooled definition (§7) is a ratio of pooled rates only; it pools nothing in any reported rate, and the per-side ratios travel with it precisely so the pooled headline can never hide a side-specific story. **The amendment's condition (v) (§8.5) adds one bilateral measurement to this picture**: both sugar sets driven at their natural, unequal counts (10 + 21). It may support a statement about the asymmetry's *size under bilateral input*, reported descriptively with CIs (§7.2); because its counts are not matched, it cannot separate the circuit-property explanation from the set-size explanation, and it supports no claim about why either. Resolution of RQ-C5 belongs to rung 6 (bilateral and unilateral stimulation at matched GRN counts), pre-registered separately.
