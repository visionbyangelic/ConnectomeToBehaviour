# Fresh vs old comparison (Part 6)

**Status: comparison complete, gate pending.** This report is the Part 6 comparison under PLAN.md section 4.1: fresh build against the old build against the paper, three-way where possible. It is written for the Part 6 HARD gate (full panel plus my decision). It does not decide the gate outcome. The decision at the end of the comparison, whether (a) the fresh build replaces the old build in the existing repo, (b) the old build stands with the fresh build published as an additional or correcting section, or (c) a hybrid, itemised outcome, is mine, at the gate, after panel review. Nothing in this report makes that decision for me, and nothing here is pushed to GitHub.

**What "old", "fresh" and "paper" mean in this report.** The old build is the first build, preserved in `v0-prototype/` and on the tag `v0-prototype-final`. The fresh build is the rebuild in `src/`, `docs/` and `results/` under the Part 5 freeze. The paper is Shiu et al. 2024, and its released outputs, above all `sugarR.parquet`. I label every number with which of the three it belongs to, and I never blur them. The old build is a debugging aid and a provenance record, not a pass or fail target; the meaningful external reference is the paper's released outputs. That caveat was adopted at Part 0 and it governs this whole comparison. The scientific value of this checkpoint is the decision log, not agreement for its own sake.

**Language discipline.** `sugarR.parquet` is the paper authors' simulation output, not animal data. Everything in the benchmark sections is model against model agreement: my implementation reproducing a reference simulation. Nothing here is biological validation, agreement with a living fly, or evidence about animal electrophysiology or behaviour. The bitter, water and sugar plus bitter results are measurements of my frozen model's own responses under conventional drives, outside the validated envelope, and I label them that way.

## 2. What is the same underneath

Before any number is compared, the foundation: the two builds are the same brain, built from the same data with the same model.

**Data.** Both builds use FlyWire v630, the same two files, the same hashes: completeness CSV SHA-256 `e6b71e17...`, connectivity parquet SHA-256 `94db8c65...`. The old build chose v630 deliberately after its first attempt with v783 failed with a `KeyError` on a published ID (`720575940620900446` present in v630, absent in v783), because the published sensory and motor IDs are compiled against v630. The fresh build made the same choice for the same reason. Section 3 gives the counts.

**Neuron identity.** I checked the ID sets directly, parsing the old `v0-prototype/src/brain/constants.py` as text (importing it pulls in Brian2, which the system Python does not have) against the fresh `data/neuron_sets/` CSVs. All four driven populations are identical, ID for ID: sugar right 21 and 21, sugar left 10 and 10, bitter 21 and 21, water 18 and 18. MN9 is the same two IDs in both builds: left `720575940660219265`, right `720575940645521262`. Population identity cannot explain any difference in this comparison, and that rules out a whole class of candidate causes at the start.

**Model constants.** The old `w_syn` is 0.275 mV, the same as fresh. The Poisson drive weight is `w_syn` times `f_poi` (250), so 68.75 mV per event, the same as fresh. The default drive rate `r_poi` is 150 Hz in both. Refractory is 2.2 ms in both, and driven neurons have it removed in both. The reset is `v = v_rst; g = 0 * mV` in the old build, the clean form. The synapse weights are `Excitatory x Connectivity` times `w_syn` in both. The old `silence()` zeroes both incoming and outgoing weights, and the old `block_output()` zeroes outgoing only, the same semantics as fresh. One latent difference, recorded in the log (D13): the old code writes those weight changes with a boolean mask (`syn.w[f'{idx} == i'] = 0 * mV`), the exact construction the fresh build proved Brian2 2.9.0 silently under-applies; the old build never called either method in any experiment, so no old number is affected by it.

**Environment.** Both builds ran Brian2 2.9.0 and numpy 1.26.4. The old build ran on Windows (conda env `na`, Python 3.11.13); the fresh build runs on Linux (Python 3.12.3). Same simulator version, different machine.

**Counting rules for the benchmark.** The old `compare_to_ground_truth` in `v0-prototype/src/brain/evaluation.py` computes Pearson correlation over the SHARED active neurons only, not the union, with Jaccard, precision and recall over the active sets. The fresh harness computes Pearson and Spearman over the union U. That difference matters in section 8, and I do not pretend the two Pearson values are computed the same way.

The suppression finding itself is the most replicated result in this comparison. Only its exact size on the right side, at the old build's rate, is unresolved.

**Candidate causes.** The drive rate differs (150 against 200 Hz), and no fresh water arm at 150 Hz was ever run, so the rate is untested for water. But the rate alone is a weak explanation on the evidence available: in sugar, moving from 150 to 200 Hz lifts MN9 L by 11 percent (83.10 to 92.57 Hz) and total spikes per trial by 24 percent; in water, the same rate change would have to lift MN9 L by 404 percent. The water cascade is weaker than the sugar cascade (fresh water: 7,564 spikes per trial and 345 active neurons, against sugar's 16,963.8 and 446 at 200 Hz), so its MN9 response may sit on a steeper part of the input-output curve, near a threshold where a modest increase in upstream drive pushes many more MN9 spikes. That is a hypothesis, not a finding. The ID sets are identical (18 water GRNs in both builds), the drive construction is identical, and the seed policies differ (old: 42 plus 17 per trial; fresh: seeds 6190 to 6219), but a seed difference cannot move a mean by a factor of five across 10 and 30 trials. The discriminating test is a fresh water arm at 150 Hz, which Part 6 does not run. There is no paper reference for water alone, so this comparison is fresh against old only, and the fresh water result is descriptive, outside the validated envelope, in its own report. The gap does not bear on the sugarR reproduction, which is the validated rung, and it does not bear on the suppression finding, which is measured against sugar. I mark it unresolved and not load-bearing for this gate, with the 150 Hz water arm named as the test that would settle it.

**Pearson: indicative agreement, not a rule verdict.** The old 0.9936 and the fresh arm's 0.99470 differ by 0.001, well inside 0.02, but they are computed over different neuron sets (the old over the 411 shared active neurons only, the fresh over the 453-neuron union), so I do not present this as a rule verdict. It is indicative only: both builds reproduce the reference's firing-rate pattern to the same third decimal place. The fresh primary's Pearson 0.99982 against the paper's 0.9998 is the protocol-matched comparison, and it passes.

**Jaccard, precision, recall: the gap is explained by the counting rules and the rate.** The old Jaccard 0.9113 sits below the fresh primary's 0.9735 and below the fresh arm's 0.9514. Two differences push it down: the old cumulative active set is built from 10 trials against the fresh 30 (a smaller union misses more of the reference's 448), and the old drive is 150 Hz, which the fresh arm shows shrinks the active set (436 against 446) and shifts rates down across the population (median minus 5.90 Hz against the reference, where the primary's median difference is minus 0.067 Hz). The fresh arm's Jaccard 0.9514 sits between the old and the primary, exactly where a rate-and-trial-count explanation puts it. Precision is essentially identical across builds (old 0.9928, fresh arm 0.9885, fresh primary 0.9888): almost every neuron either build activates is in the reference. Recall carries the gap (old 0.9174, fresh arm 0.9621, fresh primary 0.9844), which is the same trial-count and rate story told from the other side. The old build's own 100 ms benchmark (Jaccard 0.7808 time-matched, 0.7143 cumulative against 1 s) shows the same pattern at a smaller scale: shorter trials and fewer trials shrink the active set and the overlap, and the old build recorded that methodological mismatch itself in the JSON.

**Verdict for the benchmark: the fresh build reproduces the paper, and the old build reproduces the paper less completely, for reasons the comparison can name (rate and trial count). The pre-set Spearman rule has no old-side input, and I do not improvise one.**

## 11. RQ-C5: the symmetry outcome

This is the one place where the two builds contradict each other on a stated claim, rather than differing by a rate or a counting rule.

**The old build's claim.** Its README states that stimulating both left and right sugar GRNs yields exact bilateral motor symmetry (60.0 Hz against 60.0 Hz, difference 0.0 Hz), and its track records the test (`experiments/test_mn9_symmetry.py`): 1 trial of 100 ms per arm at 150 Hz. Right-only sugar (21 GRNs): MN9 L 60.0, R 20.0, asymmetry ratio 3.00. Left-only sugar (10 GRNs): MN9 L 10.0, R 20.0, asymmetry ratio 2.00, described in the track as a mirror image. Bilateral sugar (31 GRNs): MN9 L 60.0, R 60.0, difference 0.0 Hz, exact symmetry. Its conclusion: the asymmetry is a confirmed biological feature of unilateral sensory delivery, not model instability. No results file for this test exists in `v0-prototype/results/validation/`; the numbers live only in the track and the README.

**The fresh build's measurement.** The rung's bilateral secondary condition (v) drives both sugar sets together (left 10 plus right 21, at their natural unequal counts), 30 trials of 1.0 s at 200 Hz: MN9 L 93.17 [91.53, 94.80], R 64.87 [62.87, 66.80], pooled 158.03, against right-only sugar at L 93.63, R 61.47. The L greater than R asymmetry is present at a similar size under bilateral input (L minus R equals 28.30 Hz, against 32.16 Hz under right-only drive). The characterisation arm (e), bilateral sugar in the embodied loop, 20 episodes: L 93.29 [92.52, 94.02], R 66.38 [65.76, 67.01], the same pattern. The rung report is explicit that this condition reports measurements on RQ-C5's open question and resolves nothing, because the sets are at unequal counts by design.

**Why the old claim does not stand as written.** Three problems, all visible in the old build's own numbers. First, the sample: 1 trial of 100 ms per arm, where each MN9 spike is 10 Hz, so "60.0 against 60.0" is 6 spikes against 6 spikes in a single trial, and "exact symmetry" is a single-trial coincidence until replicated. Second, the left-only arm is not a mirror image of the right-only arm in magnitude: the mirror of (L 60, R 20) is (L 20, R 60), and the recorded left-only result is (L 10, R 20). What flips is only the direction of the split (R greater than L under left-only drive, L greater than R under right-only drive); the magnitudes do not mirror, which is expected, because the left population has 10 neurons against the right's 21, so left-only drive is a weaker stimulus overall. Third, the fresh 30-trial measurement at 200 Hz directly contradicts the conclusion: under bilateral drive the asymmetry persists, with the two CIs far apart. The rate differs (150 against 200 Hz) and the trial counts differ (1 against 30), so the two results are not protocol-matched, and the contradiction is not a clean falsification; but a claim of exact symmetry cannot survive a 30-trial measurement that shows a 28.30 Hz gap. Log entry D7.

**What neither build settles.** Whether the connectome itself is left-right symmetric, or whether the asymmetry comes from the unequal GRN set sizes (21 right against 10 left), or from something in the circuit. "Bilateral" in both builds means driving both populations at their natural unequal counts, so bilateral drive is itself asymmetric drive. The discriminating test is a matched-count bilateral design, equal numbers of left and right GRNs driven at the same rate, which the old plan called rung 6 and which Part 7's design owns. Until then, RQ-C5 stays open in both builds, and the old README's exact-symmetry sentence is a single-trial observation presented as a finding, not a settled result.

**Verdict for RQ-C5: unresolved in both builds; the old build's symmetry claim is not supported by its own numbers at the strength it states, and the fresh measurement points the other way.**

## 12. The closed loop: the old build has no counterpart

The largest substantive difference between the two builds is not a number. It is that the old build never ran a closed loop.

**What the old build did.** It verified the pieces separately: body physics stepping (MuJoCo 3.2.7, FlyGym `FlatTerrain` and `SingleFlySimulation` with 42 actuated joints, FlyBody FruitFly with 68 bodies and 59 actuators, each stepped 10 frames); a body wrapper (`v0-prototype/src/body/fly_body.py`) with verified stepping semantics, action space and raw contact forces; and a single unified-process compatibility diagnostic (`experiments/test_unified_closed_loop_env.py`, Session 4, 10 Nov 2026): a 50 ms brain run (521 spikes across 234 active neurons; MN9 L 4 spikes, 80.0 Hz; MN9 R 2 spikes, 40.0 Hz) with its output mapped by an explicitly labelled non-biological test mapping onto one joint (`joint_LFCoxa`, commanded from 0.3316 to 0.4916 rad, evolving to 0.3710 rad), and 500 physics steps (50.0 ms). All 7 of its evaluation checks passed, and the diagnostic's conclusion was architectural only: Brian2 and FlyGym can execute in the same process, so IPC and sockets were discarded. Its next steps, listed three times in its own track and never done, were to build `src/bridge/dn_decoder.py` and `experiments/run_closed_loop.py`. The bridge folder holds only `__init__.py`. No decoder was ever built, no closed-loop episode ever ran, and no closed-loop number exists anywhere in the old record. The old build's title, "Closed-Loop Whole-Brain Emulation of Drosophila", promises a loop its code never closed. Log entry D10.

**What the fresh build did.** Under the Part 5 freeze (tag `part5-prereg-v1`), 100 characterisation episodes, 20 per arm, all valid: arm (a) closed-loop sugar, peak E 2.043 rad [2.032, 2.051], contact in 20 of 20 episodes, MN9 L 92.60 [87.52, 95.58], R 60.97 [57.61, 63.05]; arm (b) open-loop sugar, peak E 2.030 [2.016, 2.043], contact in 20 of 20 during the scripted window and offset in all 20 after it, MN9 L 94.88 [94.00, 95.77], R 62.39 [61.68, 63.20]; arm (c) no drive, exactly zero on every measure; arm (d) sugar plus bitter, peak E 0.344 [0.317, 0.376], MN9 L 9.18 [8.68, 9.73], R 9.87 [9.43, 10.33], suppression ratio 0.121 [0.116, 0.127], no contact in any episode; arm (e) bilateral sugar, peak E 2.056 [2.055, 2.057], contact in 20 of 20. Contact duration on the registered whole-10-second definition: closed 9.0 s against open 5.5 s, difference plus 3.49 s [2.75, 3.91]. The registered endpoints show demonstrated equivalence between closed and open on peak E and on the stimulus-period contact duration, while the sustainment difference is carried by the secondary endpoints (arm (a)'s contact is gate-sustained after its script ends; arm (b)'s ends with its script). The M3 materiality band stands outside (arm (b) peak E 2.030 against the band [2.0746, 2.2746]), a discrepancy for the panel, recorded and not adjusted. The Holm-adjusted values wait for the four Part 7 lesion members.

**Verdict for the closed loop: there is no comparison to make, and I do not manufacture one.** The fresh closed loop is new work. The old build's contribution to it is the architectural finding (in-process execution works) and the body wrapper, both of which the fresh build's design inherits in spirit. The old build's closed-loop title is an overclaim in its own record, and the fresh build is the first to do what that title promised.
