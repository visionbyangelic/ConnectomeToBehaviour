## 2. What is the same underneath

Before any number is compared, the foundation: the two builds are the same brain, built from the same data with the same model.

**Data.** Both builds use FlyWire v630, the same two files, the same hashes: completeness CSV SHA-256 `e6b71e17...`, connectivity parquet SHA-256 `94db8c65...`. The old build chose v630 deliberately after its first attempt with v783 failed with a `KeyError` on a published ID (`720575940620900446` present in v630, absent in v783), because the published sensory and motor IDs are compiled against v630. The fresh build made the same choice for the same reason. Section 3 gives the counts.

**Neuron identity.** I checked the ID sets directly, parsing the old `v0-prototype/src/brain/constants.py` as text (importing it pulls in Brian2, which the system Python does not have) against the fresh `data/neuron_sets/` CSVs. All four driven populations are identical, ID for ID: sugar right 21 and 21, sugar left 10 and 10, bitter 21 and 21, water 18 and 18. MN9 is the same two IDs in both builds: left `720575940660219265`, right `720575940645521262`. Population identity cannot explain any difference in this comparison, and that rules out a whole class of candidate causes at the start.

**Model constants.** The old `w_syn` is 0.275 mV, the same as fresh. The Poisson drive weight is `w_syn` times `f_poi` (250), so 68.75 mV per event, the same as fresh. The default drive rate `r_poi` is 150 Hz in both. Refractory is 2.2 ms in both, and driven neurons have it removed in both. The reset is `v = v_rst; g = 0 * mV` in the old build, the clean form. The synapse weights are `Excitatory x Connectivity` times `w_syn` in both. The old `silence()` zeroes both incoming and outgoing weights, and the old `block_output()` zeroes outgoing only, the same semantics as fresh. One latent difference, recorded in the log (D13): the old code writes those weight changes with a boolean mask (`syn.w[f'{idx} == i'] = 0 * mV`), the exact construction the fresh build proved Brian2 2.9.0 silently under-applies; the old build never called either method in any experiment, so no old number is affected by it.

**Environment.** Both builds ran Brian2 2.9.0 and numpy 1.26.4. The old build ran on Windows (conda env `na`, Python 3.11.13); the fresh build runs on Linux (Python 3.12.3). Same simulator version, different machine.

**Counting rules for the benchmark.** The old `compare_to_ground_truth` in `v0-prototype/src/brain/evaluation.py` computes Pearson correlation over the SHARED active neurons only, not the union, with Jaccard, precision and recall over the active sets. The fresh harness computes Pearson and Spearman over the union U. That difference matters in section 8, and I do not pretend the two Pearson values are computed the same way.

But the rate alone is a weak explanation on the evidence available: in sugar, moving from 150 to 200 Hz lifts MN9 L by 11 percent (83.10 to 92.57 Hz) and total spikes per trial by 24 percent; in water, the same rate change would have to lift MN9 L by 404 percent. The water cascade is weaker than the sugar cascade (fresh water: 7,564 spikes per trial and 345 active neurons, against sugar's 16,963.8 and 446 at 200 Hz), so its MN9 response may sit on a steeper part of the input-output curve, near a threshold where a modest increase in upstream drive pushes many more MN9 spikes. That is a hypothesis, not a finding. The ID sets are identical (18 water GRNs in both builds), the drive construction is identical, and the seed policies differ (old: 42 plus 17 per trial; fresh: seeds 6190 to 6219), but a seed difference cannot move a mean by a factor of five across 10 and 30 trials. The discriminating test is a fresh water arm at 150 Hz, which Part 6 does not run. There is no paper reference for water alone, so this comparison is fresh against old only, and the fresh water result is descriptive, outside the validated envelope, in its own report. The gap does not bear on the sugarR reproduction, which is the validated rung, and it does not bear on the suppression finding, which is measured against sugar. I mark it unresolved and not load-bearing for the paper reproduction (section 15), an open condition on option (a), with the 150 Hz water arm named as the test that would settle it.

## 11. RQ-C5: the symmetry outcome

This is the one place where the two builds contradict each other on a stated claim, rather than differing by a rate or a counting rule.

**The old build's claim, in its own two wordings.** Two different documents in the old record say this differently, and I keep them apart. The preserved original front page, `v0-prototype/README-original.md` line 99, states it as a finding and as a biological consequence: "*Note on MN9 Left vs. Right Asymmetry:* The contralateral bias (MN9 Left 83.5 Hz vs. Right 58.7 Hz) is the natural biological consequence of unilateral sensory stimulation (21 right-hemisphere labellar GRNs). In empirical bilateral tests (`experiments/test_mn9_symmetry.py`), stimulating both left and right GRNs yields exact bilateral motor symmetry ($60.0\\text{ Hz}$ vs. $60.0\\text{ Hz}$, $\\Delta = 0.0\\text{ Hz}$)." The bundled front page, `v0-prototype/README.md` lines 45 to 49, repeats the 60.0 vs 60.0 numbers but frames the question as open: "**One open question v0 left behind, stated plainly:** under right-side sugar stimulation the left MN9 fires faster than the right (83.5 vs 58.7 Hz), while bilateral stimulation gives exact symmetry (60.0 vs 60.0 Hz). I did not resolve whether that asymmetry is a real property of the circuit, an artefact of the unequal left/right sensory-neuron sets in my constants (21 right vs 10 left), or a bug. The rebuild treats resolving this as a gate: no MN9 number gets published until it is answered." The bundled front page's caveat concerns the cause of the unilateral asymmetry, and it does not retract the 60/60 numbers themselves; what it does is refuse to present them as a settled finding, and it carries its own gate sentence, quoted above. The old track records the test (`experiments/test_mn9_symmetry.py`): 1 trial of 100 ms per arm at 150 Hz. Right-only sugar (21 GRNs): MN9 L 60.0, R 20.0, asymmetry ratio 3.00. Left-only sugar (10 GRNs): MN9 L 10.0, R 20.0, asymmetry ratio 2.00, described in the track as a mirror image. Bilateral sugar (31 GRNs): MN9 L 60.0, R 60.0, difference 0.0 Hz, exact symmetry. Its conclusion in the original record: the asymmetry is a confirmed biological feature of unilateral sensory delivery, not model instability. No results file for this test exists in `v0-prototype/results/validation/`; the numbers live only in the track and the README.

**The fresh build's measurement.** The rung's bilateral secondary condition (v) drives both sugar sets together (left 10 plus right 21, at their natural unequal counts), 30 trials of 1.0 s at 200 Hz: MN9 L 93.17 [91.53, 94.80], SD 4.61, R 64.87 [62.87, 66.80], SD 5.57, pooled 158.03, against right-only sugar at L 93.63, R 61.47. The L greater than R asymmetry is present at a similar size under bilateral input (L minus R equals 28.30 Hz, against 32.16 Hz under right-only drive). The characterisation arm (e), bilateral sugar in the embodied loop, 20 episodes: L 93.29 [92.52, 94.02], R 66.38 [65.76, 67.01], the same pattern. The rung report is explicit that this condition reports measurements on RQ-C5's open question and resolves nothing, because the sets are at unequal counts by design.

**Why the old claim does not stand as written.** Three problems, all visible in the old build's own numbers. First, the sample: 1 trial of 100 ms per arm, where each MN9 spike is 10 Hz, so "60.0 against 60.0" is 6 spikes against 6 spikes in a single trial, and "exact symmetry" is a single-trial coincidence until replicated. Second, the left-only arm is not a mirror image of the right-only arm in magnitude: the mirror of (L 60, R 20) is (L 20, R 60), and the recorded left-only result is (L 10, R 20). What flips is only the direction of the split (R greater than L under left-only drive, L greater than R under right-only drive); the magnitudes do not mirror, which is expected, because the left population has 10 neurons against the right's 21, so left-only drive is a weaker stimulus overall. Third, the fresh 30-trial measurement at 200 Hz directly contradicts the conclusion: under bilateral drive the asymmetry persists, with the two CIs far apart. The rate differs (150 against 200 Hz) and the trial counts differ (1 against 30), so the two results are not protocol-matched, and the contradiction is not a clean falsification; but a claim of exact symmetry cannot survive a 30-trial measurement that shows a 28.30 Hz gap. Log entry D7.

**What neither build settles.** Whether the connectome itself is left-right symmetric, or whether the asymmetry comes from the unequal GRN set sizes (21 right against 10 left), or from something in the circuit. "Bilateral" in both builds means driving both populations at their natural unequal counts, so bilateral drive is itself asymmetric drive. The discriminating test is a matched-count bilateral design, equal numbers of left and right GRNs driven at the same rate, which the old plan called rung 6 and which Part 7's design owns. Until then, RQ-C5 stays open in both builds, and the old README-original's exact-symmetry sentence is a single-trial observation presented as a finding, not a settled result.

**Reconciliation with PLAN, stated plainly because the report owes it.** PLAN section 8, RQ-C5, reads "Core, must be resolved before any MN9 number is published", and PLAN line 301 reads "Resolved at Part 3 with dedicated bilateral/unilateral runs at matched GRN counts, before any MN9 claim." No matched-count bilateral/unilateral run was done. The fresh bilateral condition (v) drove both sets at their natural unequal counts (left 10 plus right 21) at 200 Hz, so the Part 3 matched-count resolution did not happen. MN9 numbers were nevertheless published, in the Part 3 results, in the rung results, in the characterisation results, and in this report, with RQ-C5 still open. That is a breach of the bundled README's own gate sentence ("no MN9 number gets published until it is answered") and of the PLAN statements above, and I record it as such rather than softening it. D7 is therefore unresolved against a registered core question, not merely against an old-build claim, and the earlier wording in this report, "RQ-C5 was never a validated claim in either build", is corrected: RQ-C5 was a registered must-resolve question in PLAN, even though neither build validated an answer to it. How the gate treats that breach is a decision for the gate (section 17, item 4), not a finding of this comparison.

**Verdict for RQ-C5: unresolved in both builds; the old build's symmetry claim is not supported by its own numbers at the strength it states, and the fresh measurement points the other way.**

## 12. The closed loop: the old build has no counterpart

The largest substantive difference between the two builds is not a number. It is that the old build never ran a closed loop.

**What the old build did.** It verified the pieces separately: body physics stepping (MuJoCo 3.2.7, FlyGym `FlatTerrain` and `SingleFlySimulation` with 42 actuated joints, FlyBody FruitFly with 68 bodies and 59 actuators, each stepped 10 frames); a body wrapper (`v0-prototype/src/body/fly_body.py`) with verified stepping semantics, action space and raw contact forces; and a single unified-process compatibility diagnostic (`experiments/test_unified_closed_loop_env.py`, Session 4, 10 Nov 2026): a 50 ms brain run (521 spikes across 234 active neurons; MN9 L 4 spikes, 80.0 Hz; MN9 R 2 spikes, 40.0 Hz) with its output mapped by an explicitly labelled non-biological test mapping onto one joint (`joint_LFCoxa`, commanded from 0.3316 to 0.4916 rad, evolving to 0.3710 rad), and 500 physics steps (50.0 ms). All 7 of its evaluation checks passed, and the diagnostic's conclusion was architectural only: Brian2 and FlyGym can execute in the same process, so IPC and sockets were discarded. Its next steps, listed three times in its own track and never done, were to build `src/bridge/dn_decoder.py` and `experiments/run_closed_loop.py`. The bridge folder holds only `__init__.py`. No decoder was ever built, no closed-loop episode ever ran, and no closed-loop number exists anywhere in the old record. The preserved original front page carries the title verbatim (`v0-prototype/README-original.md` line 6): "### Connectome-to-Behaviour: Closed-Loop Whole-Brain Emulation of *Drosophila melanogaster*". That title promises a loop its code never closed. The bundled front page already disclaims it (`v0-prototype/README.md` lines 23 to 26): "Phase 4 — the bridge: never built. `src/bridge/` in this folder is empty apart from its `__init__.py`. The descending-neuron decoder that was supposed to turn brain activity into body movement was designed and debated in `track.md`, but it was never implemented — so v0 never ran a true closed loop, and I never claimed that it did." So the title is an overclaim in the preserved original record (README-original.md), which the bundled front page corrects; I do not present the old build as bundled as still claiming a closed loop. Log entry D10.

**What the fresh build did.** Under the Part 5 freeze (tag `part5-prereg-v1`), 100 characterisation episodes, 20 per arm, all valid: arm (a) closed-loop sugar, peak E 2.043 rad [2.032, 2.051], contact in 20 of 20 episodes, MN9 L 92.60 [87.52, 95.58], R 60.97 [57.61, 63.05]; arm (b) open-loop sugar, peak E 2.030 [2.016, 2.043], contact in 20 of 20 during the scripted window and offset in all 20 after it, MN9 L 94.88 [94.00, 95.77], R 62.39 [61.68, 63.20]; arm (c) no drive, exactly zero on every measure; arm (d) sugar plus bitter, peak E 0.344 [0.317, 0.376], MN9 L 9.18 [8.68, 9.73], R 9.87 [9.43, 10.33], suppression ratio 0.121 [0.116, 0.127], no contact in any episode; arm (e) bilateral sugar, peak E 2.056 [2.055, 2.057], contact in 20 of 20. The Holm-adjusted values wait for the four Part 7 lesion members.

**Verdict for the closed loop: there is no comparison to make, and I do not manufacture one.** The fresh closed loop is new work. The old build's contribution to it is the architectural finding (in-process execution works) and the body wrapper, both of which the fresh build's design inherits in spirit. The old build's closed-loop title is an overclaim in its preserved original record, and the fresh build is the first to do what that title promised.
