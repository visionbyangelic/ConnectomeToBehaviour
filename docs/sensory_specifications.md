## Owner's summary — in plain words

My simulated fly will have exactly these senses, and no others:

- **Touch on its feet and mouthparts.** The physics engine genuinely reports when a leg, a tarsus, or the labrum (the tip of the proboscis) touches something, and how hard. That part is real — it is the body's own contact reporting, not something I invented.
- **A "taste" signal that I construct out of that touch.** The body has no taste or smell sensors at all — the audit searched the whole package and found none. So when the labrum or the front feet touch the food object, my code turns that touch into a signal that drives the brain's sugar neurons. The touch is real; the taste is a defined stand-in, and every document in this project calls it **constructed**. It is honest because it never pretends to be chemistry: in a real fly, taste hairs on the feet and mouth transduce sugar into nerve signals; here, contact with a labelled food object plays that role, openly.
- **A mechanosensory signal from leg movement, routed into the antennal Johnston's-organ neurons — constructed, and arbitrary.** Part 2b proved the loop mechanics by feeding leg-contact events into the JON population, because JON→aDN is the one mechanosensory pathway whose neurons I have verified IDs for. Legs do not talk to antennae in a real fly; leg sensation enters through the nerve cord, which this brain-only model does not contain. I keep the channel for Core-B because it is the only feedback route I can build from verified parts, and I label it **constructed and arbitrary** wherever it appears. Part 2b is evidence that a loop *can* be built and run stably — it is **not** evidence about the connectome-to-behaviour question, and it must never be cited as such.
- **Proprioception that is recorded but not driven.** Joint angles and velocities are real body signals; in Core-B's first version they are logged for the record and do not drive any brain population. One feedback channel per behaviour keeps the closed-vs-open comparison (RQ-P3) interpretable.

Everything the fly "feels" in Part 5 passes through the specifications below: a named physical signal, a stated event definition, a stated constructed drive, a stated latency, and a provenance label on every link.

## 2. Standing rules for every embodied run

These rules bind every pathway in §§4–5, the thin slice (§6) and the qualification gate (§7). They exist because Part 2b's lessons were specific, and I am not re-learning them at Part 5 prices.

### 2.1 Bridge contract and cadence — kept at 100 ms, not split

- Brain and body advance in **100 ms chunks** on both sides, through the two-process bridge Part 2b proved (brain env: Brian2 2.9.0 / numpy 1.26.4; body env: FlyGym 2.1.1 / MuJoCo 3.16.0 / numpy 2.5.5; one JSON object per line; the driver refuses malformed replies rather than manufacturing results).
- **Decision: the cadence is NOT split by pathway.** Feeding and walk/halt both run at 100 ms. Reasons: (i) one bridge contract, already proven stable and proven dynamically neutral (Part 2b Probe 2: three 100 ms runs reproduce one 300 ms run bit-for-bit); (ii) the body's own timescales are slower than the chunk — halt takes ~0.2 s after the command zeroes (Part 1b), proboscis movements are slower still — so a finer cadence buys no physical fidelity; (iii) shorter windows starve the rate estimators (Part 2b §2: a 15 ms window on a two-neuron readout holds 0–1 spikes); Eon's 15 ms sync is a literature reference point, not a requirement (§4). Any future cadence change needs a formal amendment **and** a chunk-equivalence probe by the Part 2b method before the new cadence is trusted.
- **Latency accounting (simulated time):** the command driving body chunk *k* is decoded from brain chunk *k*, whose sensory state was set by body chunk *k−1*. Sensory feedback therefore carries exactly **one chunk (100 ms)** of simulated latency, plus estimator/filter lag where a pathway specifies one (§4.4, §5.4). Wall-clock latency is a cost figure, never part of the simulated system.

### 2.2 Harness rule — every driven population is declared and pre-compiled (Reviewer 1, Part 2b gate)

Brian2 compiles each drive's code object the first time that drive is *active*; in Part 2b, activating 146 JON drives mid-episode cost ~10 minutes of compiler time, once. Standing rule for every loop from Part 5 onward:

1. **Every brain population that any part of the run can drive is declared up front**, in the run's manifest, before the episode clock starts — stimulus populations, feedback populations, all of them. A drive that is not in the manifest may not be switched on mid-run.
2. The brain worker performs a **zero-duration warmup run with all declared drives active** before the first timed chunk, paying every compile cost outside the episode.
3. The manifest records the declared drive list; the Part 5 freeze checks the list against this document's pathway specs (feeding: §4; walk/halt: §5).

### 2.3 The settle protocol — stimulus timing is referenced to a settled baseline (Reviewer 1, Part 2b gate)

Part 2b's lesson, stated plainly: the body, placed and warmed up for 0.05 s, was still seating its legs when the episode started; chunk 0 contained ~202 leg-contact transitions, and the feedback rule honestly converted them — the closed loop ignited one chunk before the scripted pulse. The control arm absorbed it (identical settling, never converted), but stimulus timing referenced to episode start is timing referenced to a transient. From Part 5 onward:

**Warmup.** The body is placed at its spawn pose and stepped in 100 ms chunks with **zero command** (descending signal (0, 0); all decoder-driven joint targets at their initial-pose values; no stimulus, no pulse, no feedback conversion — transitions are counted, never converted, during warmup).

**Settled — definition.** The body is settled when **three consecutive chunks** each satisfy both:
- leg-contact transitions (all six legs, counted per §2.4) **≤ 2** in the chunk **[chosen]** — the settled standing state Part 2b actually measured is 0 (the open control's transitions were exactly 0 from chunk 9 onward while standing); 2 is a small allowance for numerical flicker, not a walking signal; and
- thorax height change within the chunk **≤ 0.01 mm** **[chosen]** — the body has stopped sinking/seating (Part 2b thorax heights while standing sit in a ~0.91–1.17 mm band; a settled fly's height is steady to well under this bound per chunk).

**Cap and failure.** Warmup is capped at **30 chunks (3.0 s)** **[chosen]**. If the settle criteria are not met by the cap, the episode is **invalid**: it is recorded **INVALID in the run manifest**, logged as a settle failure with its transition record, and it **counts as a failed episode**. It is never silently replaced, extended, re-warmed, or re-run under the same episode ID, and it never counts as a settled episode in any analysis (Amendment Pass 1). A pattern of settle failures is a finding about the spawn/arena, fixed by amendment to the spawn specification — never by relaxing this definition mid-run.

**Stimulus clock.** **t = 0 is the first chunk boundary after the settle criteria are met.** Every stimulus onset, offset, event time and latency in every embodied run of this project is expressed in chunks (or seconds) from that t = 0. The feedback rules of §§4–5 are **armed only from t = 0**: contact events during warmup are logged but can never drive anything. Brain-only runs (no body) have no settle phase; their t = 0 is run start, as in Part 3 — the two conventions are never mixed in one comparison.

**Feeding-arena addition.** In the feeding configuration (§4.6), the food geom is present during warmup but the fly spawns out of contact range. Any labrum–food or tarsus–food contact during warmup invalidates the episode (it means the arena geometry is wrong, which is a construction error to fix, not data).

**Across bodies — thresholds carried over, not re-derived (Amendment Pass 1).** The settle criteria above (≤ 2 transitions, ≤ 0.01 mm Δh, 3 consecutive chunks) and the 30-chunk cap were measured on NeuroMechFly in Part 2b; the Core-A body is FlyBody. Decision: **they carry over to FlyBody unchanged.** Justification: (i) the transition count is a dimensionless count of contact-flag changes, not a physical magnitude tied to one body's geometry; (ii) the height bound is on the *change* within a chunk, not on any absolute height, so a different resting height does not move it; (iii) **no unit conversion exists to get wrong** — both bodies are modelled in millimetres in FlyGym 2.1.1 (FlyBody per §4; the Part 1b NeuroMechFly demos measured travel in mm), on the same 1e-4 s physics step. Carrying over is not assuming: the §2.4 acceptance test is run **per configuration**, so FlyBody must pass the same zero-false-ignition test under the carried thresholds before Part 5 freezes; if it cannot, the thresholds are re-derived **by amendment**, with the FlyBody measurements on record — never adjusted quietly to make the test pass. The §5.3 JON thresholds (ON ≥ 8 / OFF ≤ 2) belong to the walk/halt pathway, whose body is undecided (§5): they carry with that pathway's signals if the Core-B gate keeps NeuroMechFly, and are re-derived by the FlyBody walk/halt audit if it does not — they are not asserted for FlyBody here. **Settle is brain-free:** warmup and settle use **no brain input of any kind** — no stimulus, no feedback conversion, no arm-dependent signal — so whether an episode is INVALID can never depend on the experimental arm, condition or lesion state; an invalid episode is a property of the spawn and arena only. **Reporting:** every embodied run's report states its **invalid-episode count and rate** (INVALID episodes ÷ episodes attempted) alongside its results; a run that hides its invalid rate has not reported.

### 2.4 Event-detector standard — debouncing and hysteresis everywhere

Part 2b's feedback rule was "≥1 transition in a chunk → on": a hair-trigger. No detector in Part 5 works that way. Every event detector in this document is built from the two mechanisms below, with its numbers stated in its pathway section.

- **Physics-step debounce (contact levels).** Contact state is sampled every physics step (1e-4 s) from the body's contact reporting. A contact turns **ON** only after the contact force/found flag is present for **≥ 10 consecutive steps (1 ms)**; it turns **OFF** only after **≥ 50 consecutive steps (5 ms)** of absence. Single-step flicker can neither create nor destroy an event.
- **Chunk-level hysteresis (count-based rules).** Where a rule consumes a per-chunk count, it has separate on- and off-thresholds with a hold band between them: the state changes only when the count crosses the far threshold, and holds its previous state inside the band. A detector with one threshold is a specification error.

**False ignition — operational definition (Amendment Pass 1).** A **false ignition** is either (a) any constructed feedback channel switching **ON** at any point during a resting acceptance episode — the channels being the §4.5 constructed taste event (which gates the sugar drive) and the §5.3 JON channel — or (b) any single chunk in such an episode **exceeding its channel's on-threshold**, whether or not the channel's state machine completes the switch: for the JON channel, a per-chunk transition count **≥ 8**; for the taste event, the §4.5 contact condition (qualifying contact force > 0 against the food geom) sustained for **≥ 10 consecutive physics steps** within the chunk.

**Acceptance test for the settle + detector system (pre-registered here; executed and recorded before the Part 5 freeze).** **N = 10 resting episodes** per configuration (feeding arena; walk/halt arena): body settled per §2.3, then 3.0 s (30 chunks) with **no command and no stimulus pulse**, food geom present in the feeding configuration. **Pass rule: zero false ignitions across all 10 episodes**, in the terms of the definition above. Any false ignition fails the test; the thresholds are then revisited by amendment to this document (panel-reviewed) and the test re-run — Part 5 does not freeze on a detector system that ignites a resting fly.

## 3. Stimulus specifications (encoder side)

Format per §6.1: physical event; what the body actually senses/reports; population driven; encoding; timing; calibration source; grounded vs hand-built. These specs cover both modes: **brain-only** (the stimulus is a scripted schedule, as in Part 3 — the event is declared, not sensed) and **embodied** (the stimulus is gated by the body events of §§4–5). The mechanism is identical; only the gate differs. Population ID sets are the Part 2 artefacts (`id_sets_v1`, two-way cross-checked at Part 2).

**The validated envelope (Amendment Pass 1).** Part 3 certified exactly one stimulus configuration: **sugarR — the right sugar GRN set (21 neurons) driven at 200 Hz**, brain-only, against Shiu et al.'s simulation output. That is the whole of the validated envelope. Every other stimulus configuration the loop uses — **bilateral sugar (31), left sugar (10), bitter (21), water (18), and any combination such as sugar+bitter** — is **OUTSIDE THE VALIDATED ENVELOPE**, and is labelled that way wherever it appears in this document, in the Part 5 preregistration, and in any result that uses it: the brain's response to those drives is this project's extrapolation from a validated model, not a validated behaviour of it. This applies in particular to the §4.5 constructed taste feedback, which drives **the scenario's declared sugar GRN set** (§3.1: right, left, or bilateral, declared per scenario) — so an embodied feeding episode whose declared set is anything other than right-only runs its stimulus outside the validated envelope, openly. The envelope is **not** closed by narrowing the contact rule to right-only: the §4.5 contact event is symmetric by construction, and forcing it to drive only the right set would contaminate RQ-C5 (the L/R asymmetry question) by building an asymmetry into the encoder. **Follow-up benchmark rungs for the loop's actual stimuli** — reference-output comparisons for the further stimulus configurations Part 5/7 actually use, on the Part 3 ladder model — are named future work and a Part 5 prerequisite *option* for the panel to require; they are not silently assumed to exist.

### 3.1 Sugar

- **Physical event (embodied):** labrum or front-tarsus contact with the food geom (§4.5's constructed taste event). **(Brain-only):** the scenario's declared stimulus window.
- **Body senses:** contact force on the labrum segments / front tarsus segments vs the food geom (`get_bodysegment_contact_forces`) — **physical contact**. The body senses no sweetness; the food geom's identity as "sugar" is a scenario-script label joined to the contact event (Part 1b §4: chemistry is a defined input event).
- **Population driven:** the scenario's declared sugar GRN set — right (21 neurons), left (10), or bilateral (31) — declared per scenario in the Part 5 preregistration, never chosen mid-run. (Part 3's validated configuration is right-only, 21.)
- **Encoding:** Poisson drive at **200 Hz** per neuron while the event is ON; off otherwise. Rate settled at Part 3 (Amendment Pass 3): the reference file's driven GRNs fire at 196.8 Hz mean against 0.0 Hz controls, and the 150 Hz arm's driven GRNs fired at 147.95 Hz — 200 Hz is the measured generating rate of the benchmark this project's brain is validated against.
- **Timing:** drive state for brain chunk *k+1* is the event state at the end of body chunk *k* (§2.1 latency). Brain-only: per the scenario schedule, referenced to run start.
- **Calibration source:** Part 3 preregistration/results. **Grounded:** population identity (upstream/literature, cross-checked). **Hand-built:** the Poisson encoding itself and the contact→drive join (encoder, class **D** at the join; the resulting GRN/MN9 dynamics are **E1**).

### 3.2 Bitter

- **Physical event:** contact with a geom the scenario script labels "bitter source" (embodied), or the declared window (brain-only). The body reports the same contact as for sugar; **which** geom is bitter exists only in the script — stated plainly, because bitter suppression (RQ-C2) must come from the brain's response to the bitter GRN drive, never from a body-side or decoder-side rule (§6.2: no hidden overrides).
- **Population driven:** bitter GRN set (21 neurons). **Encoding:** Poisson, **200 Hz** while ON **[chosen convention]** — the project's settled GRN drive rate (Part 3); bitter's own generating rate was never discrimination-checked against a reference file, and the §10 sensitivity obligation covers the choice. Timing and latency as §3.1.

### 3.3 Water

- **Physical event:** contact with a script-labelled "water source" geom, or the declared window. **Population driven:** water GRN set (18 neurons). **Encoding:** Poisson, **200 Hz** while ON (same convention as §3.2).
- The **Ir94e set (18 neurons)** is extracted and on record but is **not driven** in Core-A/Core-B v1 **[chosen]**: the feeding readouts this project validates against were defined on the GRN sets above; adding a second water-related drive is an amendment option if a Part 5/7 question needs it, not a silent extra input.

### 3.4 Antennal mechanosensory / dust

- **Physical event:** a dust particle (a small sphere geom, radius 0.05 mm **[chosen]**, placed by the scenario so that it contacts an antenna) touching an antennal segment. **Body senses:** per-segment contact force on the antennal segments (`get_bodysegment_contact_forces`) — **physical contact**, debounced per §2.4.
- **Population driven:** JON-all (146 neurons: JON-CE 70, JON-F 60, JON-D_m 16). **Encoding:** Poisson at **150 Hz** while the event is ON — the upstream default drive rate, and the rate Part 2b's probes characterised (aDN1 22 Hz, aDN2-left 24 Hz sustained at 150 Hz; 34 Hz each at 200 Hz, recorded there as the sensitivity fact).
- **Honesty label:** this is a **constructed sensory signal with a biological counterpart** — Johnston's organ *is* the fly's antennal mechanosensor, so the routing is anatomically sane; the transduction (MuJoCo contact force → Poisson drive at a chosen rate) is still my construction. This label is **different** from the leg-contact channel of §5.3, which has no biological counterpart, and the two must never be described in the same words.
- **Scope:** neural level only. Body-level grooming failed the Part 1b audit (no controller, no trajectory, no reachability evidence) and stays out of Core-B; this spec exists for RQ-C6's neural-level question and for the walk/halt feedback design (§5), not for a grooming behaviour claim.

## 4. Feeding pathway — Core-A, on FlyBody

Body: **FlyBody** (§0.4 decision), the model shipping inside FlyGym 2.1.1, pinned and hashed at the qualification gate (§7.1). Upstream labels FlyBody support in FlyGym **"experimental"** — that word travels with the body wherever this project describes it. Units mm, physics step 1e-4 s, chunk 100 ms (§2.1).

### 4.1 The registered feeding chain (end to end, as §0.4 requires)

```
sugar stimulus (§3.1, encoder) → sugar GRNs fire (brain) → … connectome … → MN9 L/R fire
→ [rate estimator §4.2] → [engineered mapping §4.3] → proboscis joint targets (5 DoFs, §4.3)
→ body physics (published FlyBody actuators) → proboscis extends → labrum contacts food geom
→ [constructed taste event §4.5] → sugar GRN drive sustained (§3.1) → loop closes
```

Every link's provenance is tabled in §4.7. Two honesty rules from §0.4 bind everything below: **the extension angle is a function of the decoder mapping, not independent evidence about the connectome**; and the body, its version and every parameter are **frozen identically across all experimental arms** once qualified.

### 4.2 Rate estimator — MN9 L/R → population rate (FIXED here)

- **Readout:** the two MN9 neurons (ID set `mn9.csv`, order left-then-right per the Part 2 manifest), spike counts from the frozen model's spike monitor. MN9 is the biological feeding readout (§6.2); nothing else is read for the feeding command.
- **Window:** trailing **500 ms** ending at the current chunk boundary, per side: r_L = (spikes in window)/0.5 s, r_R likewise. **[Chosen]** — at the validated sugar response (Part 3 primary: L 92.57 Hz, R 62.40 Hz) a 500 ms window holds ~46 and ~31 spikes (Poisson CV ≈ 15–18%); the 100 ms chunk window would hold ~9 and ~6 (CV ≈ 33–41%), too noisy to aim a joint at. The window is a boxcar (uniform weights) — no hidden shaping.
- **Pooled rate:** r = r_L + r_R (the decoder commands one proboscis, not two; the L/R split is preserved in the logs and in the §5.2 neural metrics, and RQ-C5 owns the asymmetry question — the decoder does not get to vote on it).
- **Smoothing:** one exponential filter on the pooled rate, τ = **0.2 s**, exact discretisation per chunk: r_filt ← r_filt + (1 − e^(−0.1/0.2))·(r − r_filt), i.e. α = 0.3935, initialised to 0 at t = 0 (§2.3 clock). **[Chosen]** — τ matches Part 2b's decoder filter, keeping the latency accounting comparable across parts; the boxcar window already smooths, and stacking a second heavy filter would add lag without adding honesty.
- **No other transformation.** No per-side weighting, no threshold inside the estimator, no baseline subtraction. What MN9 does is what the estimator reports.
- **Startup (Amendment Pass 1).** The 500 ms window spans 5 chunks, so for chunks 0–4 the window is necessarily partial. Rule: in those chunks the rate divides the spike count accumulated since t = 0 by the **elapsed time** (0.1 s per completed chunk), not by 0.5 s — a constant-rate response therefore reads at its true value from the first chunk instead of ramping up as an artefact of a zero-padded window; from chunk 5 on, the full trailing 500 ms applies. The EMA's initial state is **r_filt = 0 at t = 0** (as specified above): no priming spikes are invented and no pre-roll is borrowed from warmup, during which the brain is not driven at all (§2.3).

### 4.3 Engineered mapping — rate → joint targets (form FIXED here; one constant to Part 5 calibration)

**Extension command:** e = clip( (r_filt − r_lo) / (r_hi − r_lo), 0, 1 ), with:
- **r_lo = 5 Hz (pooled), FIXED here.** Reason: MN9's verified floor is silence — undriven, the network fires nothing (Part 2 tests), and in Part 2b's JON-only runs MN9 stayed silent. 5 Hz pooled sits just above that verified zero and far below any sugar-driven response (~155 Hz pooled in Part 3), so e = 0 at rest is a measured property, not a hope.
- **r_hi — assigned to Part 5 calibration, fitting rule stated here:** r_hi is the **95th percentile of the pooled filtered MN9 rate (r_filt as defined in §4.2) across the Part 5 calibration set's sugar-only embodied episodes** (the calibration set is defined in writing in the Part 5 preregistration, per §7.3, before any fitting). r_hi is computed **once**, then frozen and hashed with the decoder at the Part 5 gate. Sanity bound, stated in advance: if calibration yields **r_hi ≤ r_lo + 10 Hz**, the decoder is rejected and rebuilt under §7.3's rejection rule — a mapping whose full-extension point sits within 10 Hz of its floor is not a decoder, it is a switch glued to noise. No other constant in this section is calibration-assigned.

**Calibration/evaluation separation (binding; Amendment Pass 1).** The separation rule is fixed here, not by Part 5: (i) every Part 5 episode is designated **calibration** or **evaluation** in writing in the Part 5 preregistration **before either kind is run**; (ii) **only designated calibration sugar-only episodes may determine r_hi** — no other episode, of any kind, contributes to fitting it; (iii) **evaluation episodes never contribute to fitting or revising r_hi**, in whole or in part, under any re-analysis; (iv) once computed, **r_hi is recorded and hashed before any evaluation episode runs**, and the hash travels with the frozen decoder; (v) if r_hi fails the sanity bound above (r_hi ≤ r_lo + 10 Hz), the decoder is **rejected** — there is no rescue by changing the fitting rule, the calibration dataset, or the calibration/evaluation split after the fact; a rebuilt decoder starts again under this same rule. Part 5 chooses the scenario list and the numerical split (how many episodes of each kind); it never chooses, weakens or re-opens this separation rule.

For each driven joint j, with θ_rest(j) and θ_ext(j) as defined in §4.4:

- **Rostrum pitch, haustellum pitch:** target(j, e) = θ_rest(j) + e · (θ_ext(j) − θ_rest(j)). The Q2-measured extension sign enters **only through θ_ext(j)**: θ_ext(j) is the **achievable extreme** in direction s_j from θ_rest(j) (§4.4): the clamped-map achieved angle when the servo command sits at the published range limit in that direction (Amendment Pass 6 — rostrum pitch −0.954 rad, haustellum pitch −1.223 rad under the compiled map; measured at the reach-curve calibration as −0.9550 / −1.2237) — equivalently, target(j, e) = θ_rest(j) + s_j · e · |θ_ext(j) − θ_rest(j)|. The sign is never a free factor in the mapping and never chosen per episode or per result; it is read from the frozen gate record.
- **Labrum pitch (both sides, symmetric):** target(e) = θ_rest + e · (θ_ext,lab − θ_rest), where the labrum's extension extreme θ_ext,lab is its **achievable extreme +0.2625 rad** (Amendment Pass 6: the clamped-map achieved angle at the published command limit, q̂_eff(1.05) = 0.25 × 1.05; measured 0.2623/0.2624). Labellar opening is part of the engineered mapping (class **D**) — in a real fly it is motor-driven too, but here its shape is my line, and the ledger says so.
- **Safety margin (superseded by Amendment Pass 6):** θ_ext was originally the gate-determined extension end pulled **0.05 rad** inside the published limit, so the mapping would never command a joint onto its hard limit. The achievable-extreme definition supersedes it: the intended angles now never require commands beyond the published range at all, and at e = 1 the command equals the range limit by construction while the **achieved** angle remains far inside the joint's mechanical range (rostrum −0.954 of [−1.24, 0.183]; labrum 0.2625 of [−0.005, 1.05]) — the joint itself never approaches its limit stop. Q2's sweep, which commands the full raw range by design, is exempt as before. **Stated plainly: the 0.05 rad safety margin no longer applies to extension targets.**
- **Haustellum yaw: held at θ_rest for all e.** **[Chosen]** — no lateral steering in Core-A. The only lateral signal in the circuit is the MN9 L/R asymmetry, which is RQ-C5's open question; building a yaw decoder on an unresolved asymmetry would pre-judge it. Yaw is recorded in the ledger as *held*, class D.
- **Neck joints** (`c_thorax-c_head-{pitch,roll,yaw}`, ranges [−0.5, 0.3] / [−3, 3] / [−0.2, 0.2]): **not among the five proboscis DoFs and not decoded** — held at their settled values throughout Core-A feeding, recorded as held (class D). If a later question needs head movement, it arrives by amendment with its own mapping, not by drift.

**The static map and the feedforward (Amendment Pass 3, as revised).** FlyBody's position servos fight the joints' own passive springs: at steady state a joint settles where servo force and spring force balance, **q̂(ctrl) = (kp·ctrl + ks·ref)/(kp + ks)**, with kp the actuator gain, ks the joint stiffness and ref the spring reference — **all three read from the compiled artefact, not the YAML files (Amendment Pass 5)**: kp from the compiled actuator gainprm, ks from the compiled jnt_stiffness, ref from the compiled **qpos_spring** (the field that defines the spring reference; qpos0 is the model's default pose — the two coincide for this artefact and both are recorded at Q0). For this pinned artefact: kp 10 (head group) and 1 (antenna-labrum group), ks 3 for all five proboscis DoFs, and **ref = 0 for all five** — `joints.yaml` lists a springref of 0.8 for the two pitch joints, but the package's construction does not carry it into the compiled model: `FlyBody.add_joints` sets each joint's spring reference from the construction's neutral-pose lookup (0.0 for every joint absent from it; the shipped lookup holds only three wing joints). The first gate re-run failed Q2 on exactly that divergence (offset (3/13)·0.8 = 0.1846 rad) before the diagnostic isolated it. Stated as provenance: **the compiled body departs from the published FlyBody model in its passive spring rests** — the YAML's 42 springref entries are replaced at construction by neutral-pose values — **while its joint ranges, servo gains and joint stiffness are the published values**; wherever this document calls a parameter "published", the spring rests are the stated exception. The map used everywhere in this document is the **clamped** map **q̂_eff(ctrl) = clamp(q̂(ctrl), range_min, range_max)** over the published joint range. The clamp is retained as part of the rule — the map is defined over the published range in general — but under the compiled parameters it binds nowhere in the proboscis: for the pitch joints q̂(ctrl) = (10/13)·ctrl, giving rostrum [−0.954, +0.141] rad over its ctrl range, inside [−1.24, 0.183] throughout; for the labrum (kp 1, ks 3, ref 0) the achieved angle is 0.25 × ctrl. The published body cannot track raw targets, and no criterion in this project pretends otherwise. The chain therefore commands through the map's inverse: the mapping above fixes the intended **achieved** angles, and the ctrl actually written is **ctrl = ((kp + ks)·θ_intended − ks·ref)/kp**, clipped to the published range. **The clip is the published joint range**: `actuators.yaml` declares ctrllimited with no separate ctrlrange, and in the compiled model (Demo C construction) every proboscis actuator's ctrlrange equals its joint range exactly — verified against the compiled model and re-read and recorded at Q0 as a premise check: **a mismatch at Q0 stops the gate**; the specification never quietly adopts whichever range would produce a pass. Where the clip binds, the achieved angle is q̂_eff at the clip, and the achievable envelope — E_max included — is whatever the map and the ranges jointly allow, measured at the qualification gate. The map is computed from the compiled artefact's parameters only and is never fitted to measured angles. (Within the proboscis, the published force caps do not bind inside the clamped ranges — worst case ≈5.5 against a cap of 10 — so a measured steady state departing from the clamped map is a finding for Q2's map-residual criterion to catch, not an expectation.) In particular, the labrum's 1.00 rad full-open figure below is an intended **achieved** angle: its inverse-map ctrl clips at the published maximum, so the achieved full-open is q̂_eff(1.05) ≈ 0.26 rad, and the gate measures the real value; the §4.4 expected-extension table's E_max is likewise the **achieved** E at e = 1, measured at Q3. **At e = 0 (r ≤ r_lo) the chain writes ctrl = 0 to the five proboscis DoFs**; θ_rest is the settled pose under that command (§4.4's reference pose), not an intended angle of 0 — for e > 0 the intended achieved angles interpolate from θ_rest and the ctrl written is the inverse-map value of the intended angle. **The frozen object for Part 5 is this analytic rule** (formula + pinned parameters); Q2's empirical map is its audit, and disagreement beyond the 0.05 rad tolerance at any point is Q2 failing under §7.7 — the empirical map never replaces the analytic one, which would be fitting. Finally, stated for every later document: **extension amplitudes in this project are servo-limited model outputs, not physiological quantities** — the servos reach only part of the published ranges (rostrum pitch down to ≈ −0.95 rad of its [−1.24, 0.183] range; labrum full-open ≈ 0.26 rad of [−0.005, 1.05]) — and E_max is reported at the gate and the freeze both in radians and as a fraction of the extension the raw published extremes would give.

**The mapping, stated completely (Amendment Pass 1).** So that no implementer has to assemble the rule from pieces: the normalised command is e = clip((r_filt − r_lo)/(r_hi − r_lo), 0, 1) — e = 0 whenever r_filt ≤ r_lo, e = 1 whenever r_filt ≥ r_hi, and e rises linearly between them; nothing outside [0, 1] is ever commanded. Each driven joint's target is its own linear interpolation between its two registered endpoints: the pitch joints per the first bullet above (sign entering through θ_ext(j)), the labrum joints per the second bullet. **Each joint's neutral/reference target is its settled rest value θ_rest(j)** (§4.4, measured per episode at t = 0): at e = 0 every driven joint's target is exactly θ_rest(j), so zero command reproduces the settled pose by construction. **Haustellum yaw and all three neck joints are not interpolated at all: they remain at their registered settled values for the whole episode, in every experimental arm** — no arm, condition or lesion state changes a held joint's target.

**Labrum adhesion rule (engineered, class D):** FlyBody ships labrum adhesion actuators by default (`add_labrum=True`) — a physical latch modelling labellar grip. Rule: adhesion **engages** in the first chunk after the labrum–food contact event (§4.5) turns ON; it **releases** after the contact event has been OFF for **2 consecutive chunks** **[chosen]** (release hysteresis, so a one-chunk contact flicker cannot drop the food mid-event). The adhesion state is logged per chunk; it is part of the registered chain because it changes contact persistence, hence the feedback.

**Bridge tests the mapping must pass before the loop is trusted** (§6.2, applied; executed inside the qualification gate's Q6, §7.4): zero MN9 rate → e = 0 → targets = rest (zero-in/zero-out); the rate→extension calibration curve recorded over a synthetic rate ladder; monotone non-decreasing extension along the ladder; no code path from any bitter signal to the mapping (bitter suppression, if it appears, comes from the brain or not at all).

### 4.4 Measurement definitions — reference pose, extension angle, onset/offset (FIXED here; used by §5.2 metrics and by §7)

- **Reference (rest) pose θ_rest(j):** the measured angle of joint j at t = 0 — i.e. at the end of the §2.3 settle, under zero command — recorded per episode. The compiled model's spring reference (qpos_spring) is 0 for all five proboscis joints (Amendment Pass 5; `joints.yaml`'s springref of 0.8 for the pitch joints is not carried into the compiled model), recorded in the gate record at Q0; the *measurement* reference is the settled pose, because that is the pose every episode actually starts from. Extension is always measured from the episode's own θ_rest.
- **Extension direction:** which end of each pitch joint's range is "extension" is an empirical property of the body, determined once at the qualification gate (Q2, §7.3): the direction that moves the labrum tip away from the head and toward the food plane. The gate records the sign s_j ∈ {+1, −1} per pitch joint; θ_ext(j) is then the achievable extreme in that direction (§4.3, Amendment Pass 6). The sign is a gate output, frozen with the body — it is not re-derived per episode and never chosen per result.
- **Extension angle:** E(t) = s_ros·(θ_ros(t) − θ_rest,ros) + s_haus·(θ_haus(t) − θ_rest,haus), in radians, sampled per chunk from `get_joint_angles`. (The two-segment pitch sum — the §5.2 body-level feeding metric's angle.)
- **Event onset:** the first chunk where E ≥ **0.10 rad** and E stays ≥ 0.10 rad for **≥ 2 consecutive chunks**; onset time = that first chunk's start. The 0.10 rad onset threshold is FIXED here.
- **Event offset:** the first later chunk where E < **θ_off** for **≥ 2 consecutive chunks**, where **θ_off = 3 × ε_res** **[chosen multiple]** and **ε_res** is the residual error measured at the qualification gate: the largest of (a) Q2's per-point map-relative steady-state errors (§7.3, Amendment Pass 3) and (b) Q3's post-retraction residuals (§7.4, E's distance from the pre-cycle reference after retraction). The offset threshold is therefore **not a bare constant**: its formula is fixed here, its numerical value is computed once from the gate record and **frozen as a QUALIFICATION GATE OUTPUT** (§8 register). Three different quantities in this document sit near 0.05 rad and now carry three distinct names, because they do three different jobs: the **range safety margin** (0.05 rad, formerly §4.3 — how far inside the published joint limits the mapping's intended achieved angles could reach; **retired for extension targets by Amendment Pass 6**, whose achievable-extreme definition replaces it — Q2's measurement sweep was always exempt by design, commanding the nine raw points across the full published range, endpoints included, because its job is to measure the map at the extremes), the **gate tracking tolerance** (0.05 rad, §7 — how accurately the body must follow commands at the qualification gate), and the **offset threshold θ_off** (this section — where a finished extension event is declared over). None may be substituted for another.
- **Band guard (Amendment Pass 1, added at Reviewer 1's re-check).** θ_off must lie **strictly below** the onset threshold — the hysteresis band exists only if θ_off < 0.10 rad. The gate tolerances permit ε_res up to 0.05 rad, which would put θ_off as high as 0.15 rad, so this is a real condition, not a formality: when the gate record is frozen, θ_off is computed and checked, and **if 3 × ε_res ≥ 0.10 rad, θ_off is not frozen, the qualification gate FAILS at this step, and the failure returns to the panel by formal amendment**. The multiplier, the onset threshold and the gate tolerances are none of them adjusted to make the band fit.
- The onset/offset pair is the hysteresis band — wide enough that estimator noise (CV ≈ 15% on a ~155 Hz pooled rate, filtered) cannot strobe an event, narrow against the commanded range (E at e = 1 is of order 1 rad or more, measured at Q3).
- **Duration** = offset − onset. **Latency** (for a stimulus-referenced event) = onset − stimulus onset, both on the §2.3 clock. **Peak extension** = max E over [onset, offset]. **Mean extension** = mean E over [onset, offset]. An excursion that never sustains 2 chunks above 0.10 rad is **not an event**; it is logged as a sub-threshold excursion, not silently dropped and not promoted.
- **Endpoint hierarchy (Amendment Pass 1).** For the sugar+bitter conflict analyses (RQ-C2 family), the **graded endpoints — peak E and mean E — are primary**; the **thresholded endpoints — onset and duration — are secondary**, because they inherit the quantisation of the onset/offset thresholds while the graded endpoints use the whole excursion. A conflict claim made on a thresholded endpoint alone, against the graded endpoints, is reported as threshold-conditional, in those words.

**What E sums, stated exactly (Amendment Pass 6, Reviewer 1's register reconciliation):** E is the sum of the two pitch joints' **angle excursions from the same run's settled rest pose** — E = s_ros·(θ_ros − θ_rest,ros) + s_haus·(θ_haus − θ_rest,haus), θ from the actuated-joint coordinates — not a sum of angles from qpos zero and not a segment-orientation measure. The raw-extremes E is defined identically, with the published range ends in place of the achieved angles: Σ_j s_j·(range_end_j − θ_rest,j) = **2.8258 rad** at the recorded rests (the naive 1.24 + 1.59 = 2.83 measures from qpos zero and overstates by the same 0.00415 rad rest offset; an earlier gate summary's 2.8264 could not be reproduced from the recorded inputs and is superseded by the artefact value 2.8258). E_max / raw-extremes = 2.1746 / 2.8258 ≈ 0.7695.

**Expected extension by pooled rate — pre-registered table (Amendment Pass 1).** At steady state the EMA converges (r_filt = r, the pooled MN9 rate), so the registered ramp predicts E(r) = e(r) · E_max with e(r) = clip((r − 5)/(r_hi − 5), 0, 1) (§4.3's equation, unchanged) and **E_max** = E at e = 1 = s_ros·(θ_ext,ros − θ_rest,ros) + s_haus·(θ_ext,haus − θ_rest,haus) — a **qualification gate output** (signs and extremes frozen at Q2; θ_rest as settled at the gate). The table's form and rate points are fixed NOW; it is completed numerically at the Part 5 freeze, when both E_max (gate) and r_hi (Part 5 calibration, §4.3) exist, and the completed table is hashed with the decoder:

| Pooled MN9 rate r (Hz) | e(r) | Expected E (rad) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 25 | (25 − 5)/(r_hi − 5) | e · E_max |
| 50 | (50 − 5)/(r_hi − 5) | e · E_max |
| 75 | (75 − 5)/(r_hi − 5) | e · E_max |

Any Part 5 measured extension curve is reported against this table; a systematic departure is a finding about the mapping or the body, not a licence to refit the table.

### 4.5 Constructed taste feedback — labrum/tarsus contact → sugar drive (FIXED here)

- **Physical signals (physical contact):** per-segment contact forces from `get_bodysegment_contact_forces` for (i) the two labrum segments and (ii) the front-leg tarsus segments, each tested against the **food geom** only (contacts with ground or other geoms do not count). Sampled every physics step.
- **Event definition:** the taste-contact event turns ON when either labrum segment, or any front tarsus segment, sustains contact force > 0 against the food geom for **≥ 10 consecutive physics steps (1 ms)**; it turns OFF after **≥ 50 consecutive steps (5 ms)** with no qualifying contact (§2.4 debounce). Event state is sampled at chunk boundaries: the drive for brain chunk *k+1* uses the event state at the end of body chunk *k*.
- **Constructed signal:** while the event is ON, the scenario's sugar stimulus (§3.1) is driven — the event *gates* the pre-registered stimulus definition rather than inventing a new one. The set driven is the scenario's declared sugar GRN set; unless that set is right-only, this stimulus is **OUTSIDE THE VALIDATED ENVELOPE** (§3, Amendment Pass 1) and is labelled so in the run manifest. While OFF, the sugar drive is off (unless the scenario separately declares a scripted stimulus window, in which case both gates are logged and the scenario says which is which — open-loop arms depend on this distinction, RQ-P3).
- **Provenance, in the required words:** the contact is **physical contact**; the signal it produces is a **constructed sensory signal**; it is **not biological gustatory transduction**, and no document, figure or talk from this project may call it taste *sensation* without the word "constructed" attached. The join (contact → GRN drive) is encoder, class **D**; the GRN→MN9 dynamics it gates are **E1**.

### 4.6 The food geom and arena (feeding configuration)

- **Food geom:** a flat cylinder (MuJoCo `cylinder`), radius **0.5 mm**, height **0.05 mm**, non-moving, named in the arena file **[chosen dimensions]** — large enough for both labella and the front tarsi, small enough that contact is an event, not a neighbourhood.
- **Substrate (Amendment Pass 6, as revised):** the food disc sits on a **fixed pedestal** — a cylinder of radius 0.5 mm and height **0.28 mm**, same centre as the disc, so the disc's top surface is at **T = ground + 0.33 mm**. The height follows a stated rule from a stated measurement, never a search. The reach R is defined by the **reach-curve protocol** (kinematics only, no food: the settled body, e swept 0→1 through the registered chain, the labrum's lowest surface point measured by MuJoCo geom-distance to the ground plane; the centre-minus-bounding-radius method is expressly **not** used — for these elongated geoms the bounding sphere overstates the extent below the centre by ≈0.086 mm, larger than the press window). The calibration (24 Nov 2026, `REACH-CURVE-CALIBRATION.md`) measured **R = 0.3083 mm** quasi-statically; the third execution's faster ramp protocol read ≈0.296 mm; the bracket **[0.296, 0.308] mm** is recorded. **Rule: T is the smallest multiple of 0.01 mm that is ≥ R + 0.02 mm** — 0.3083 + 0.02 = 0.3283 → **T = 0.33 mm** — a press at e = 1 of 0.022 mm (sweep R) to 0.034 mm (ramp R), inside the panel's bound R ≤ T ≤ R + 0.05 under both readings. **Decision rule, pre-registered with its outcome:** the contact-onset fraction f_c(T) — the achieved E/E_max at which the descending labrum surface crosses T, read off the reach curve's **measured E/E_max axis** (E is an achieved quantity, so f_c is independent of which e definition drove the sweep; Q5(vi) is measured on the same axis) — is **0.884** at T = 0.33, the conservative (latest-onset) reading; since f_c ≤ 0.95 the default stands (had f_c exceeded 0.95, T would move to R + 0.05 and f_c be recomputed; had it still exceeded 0.95, the geometry would offer no usable graded range and the Part 1b rescope would apply). Plausibility cap: the pedestal height (0.28 mm) is below the panel's 0.6 mm cap (≈ a quarter of the 2.34 mm standing thorax height). **Sensitivity, from the same curve (no extra runs):** f_c = 0.948 / 0.901 / 0.858 / 0.816 at T = 0.316 / 0.326 / 0.336 / 0.346 mm; onset at E/E_max = 0.60 / 0.80 / 0.95 would need T = 0.405 / 0.350 / 0.316 mm. The pedestal's footprint is registered: radius 0.5 mm, centred at the §4.6 placement point (0.6 mm in front of the labrum tip's settled ground projection). The pedestal is arena construction, not a body parameter; it stands under the food disc only, intersects no other geom, and changes nothing about the body, the chain, the food's contact properties or the §4.5 event definition. **Labelling:** the pedestal is an engineered arena parameter (class **D**); every claim made from the feeding configuration states that this body, as registered with the neck held, cannot contact ground-level food, and that feeding is demonstrated on a raised substrate. **This amendment is one-time:** if Q5 fails under it, no further pedestal or geometry adjustment is permitted, and the choice between the chain amendment and the Part 1b rescope **returns to the panel** — any move to the chain amendment is a fresh pre-registered amendment (it re-registers §4.3 and §4.4), not an in-gate edit, and if no panel majority forms the default is the rescope. (The clause's voting history, including Reviewer 3's dissent in favour of an automatic rescope, is recorded in the Amendment history.)
- **Placement:** centre on the ground at **0.6 mm in front of the labrum tip's settled ground projection** (measured at the end of the §2.3 settle, per episode configuration) **[chosen starting value]**. Reachability is not assumed: qualification step Q5 (§7.5) verifies contact at full extension; if 0.6 mm is unreachable, the offset is adjusted **once, at Q5, as arena construction** (it is not a body parameter), the final value is recorded in the gate record, and that value is frozen for Part 5. Contact during warmup invalidates the episode (§2.3).
- Bitter/water source geoms, when a scenario needs them, are cylinders of the same dimensions, script-labelled per §3.2–3.3, placed symmetrically; their placement values are scenario parameters declared in the Part 5 preregistration under this same construction rule.

## 5. Walk/halt pathway — Core-B

**Body dependency, stated before anything else:** Part 1b demonstrated walk/halt on **NeuroMechFly only**, and §0.4 explicitly does **not** commit Core-B's body. This section is therefore written against the signals the audit verified on NeuroMechFly (legs-only locomotion configuration: 42 position actuators + 6 adhesion; the packaged `HybridTurningController` taking a 2-vector descending signal; per-leg ground-contact sensors). **If the Core-B gate chooses FlyBody, every signal named here must be re-verified by a FlyBody walk/halt audit before this section binds** — the section's event definitions and labels carry over, its sensor names do not automatically. Core-B also runs after Core-A's gate; nothing here is needed for Part 5. **This section is an INTERFACE SPEC ONLY** (Amendment Pass 1): it fixes the signals, events and measurements the walk/halt loop will use, but the loop as specified has **no brain-side halting mechanism** (see §5.5), so §5 does not yet specify a working closed walk/halt behaviour — it binds as a behaviour spec only when a halt source is named and pre-registered.

### 5.1 Forward chain (as audited; decoder internals are Part 7b's, not Part 4's)

Brain descending readout (aDN1 + aDN2-left — the only descending sets with verified IDs, Part 2) → decoder → **2-vector descending signal (left, right)** → packaged `HybridTurningController` → CPG → 42 leg position targets + adhesion timing (Part 1b §2). Provenance note the ledger must carry (§6.3): with the packaged controller in the loop, a named part of the behaviour is generated by an **upstream controller** — the CPG stepping pattern is the body's, not the connectome's; the connectome-side claim extends only to the descending signal. (The alternative interface — decoder emitting all 42 targets directly — exists per the audit and remains an option the Core-B gate may take; this spec's sensory side is identical either way.) Part 2b's toy decoder (pooled rate → EMA → symmetric vector) is the provisional instance of this chain; its symmetric-output constraint stands until a right-side descending set exists: the extracted sets contain aDN1 (unpaired) and aDN2-**left** only, so any left/right asymmetry in the command would be invented, and none is specified here.

### 5.2 Locomotion feedback — signals

- **Per-leg ground contact (physical contact):** the six MuJoCo leg contact sensors (`ground_contact_{leg}_leg`, via `get_ground_contact_info`), found flag sampled every physics step. Per-chunk **transition count** = total flag changes across all six legs within the chunk (the Part 2b counting rule, retained).
- **Front-tarsus / labrum contact with the food geom (physical contact):** per-segment contact forces (`get_bodysegment_contact_forces`) on the front tarsus segments and the head/labrum segments vs the food geom, debounced per §2.4 — the halt-triggering event of §5.5. (On NeuroMechFly these segments exist and report contact forces; there is no proboscis actuation and no labrum adhesion on this body, and none is claimed.)
- **Proprioception (recorded, not driven):** joint angles and velocities for all leg DoFs (`get_joint_angles` / `get_joint_velocities`), logged per chunk. In Core-B v1 proprioception drives **no** brain population **[chosen]** — one driven feedback channel (§5.3) keeps the closed-vs-open comparison interpretable; a proprioceptive drive, if ever wanted, arrives by amendment with its population and encoding stated.

### 5.3 The JON feedback channel — CONSTRUCTED AND ARBITRARY (Reviewer 1's label, carried verbatim)

- **Rule (with the §2.4 hysteresis Part 2b lacked):** the JON drive for brain chunk *k+1* is **ON** if chunk *k*'s transition count is **≥ 8**; once ON it stays ON until a chunk with count **≤ 2**; counts 3–7 hold the previous state. **[Chosen thresholds]** — settled standing measures 0 (§2.3); stepping produces transitions continuously and in far greater numbers (Part 2b's feedback chunks were transition-rich throughout, and its standing control produced exactly 0); the 8/2 band exists so a single stray flicker can neither ignite nor kill the channel, which is precisely the failure the old ≥1 rule invited. The drive is armed only from t = 0 (§2.3) — settle transitions can no longer ignite anything.
- **Encoding:** JON-all (146 neurons), Poisson **150 Hz** while ON (§3.4's rate and population).
- **The label, in full, to be carried wherever this channel is used or cited:** this channel is a **constructed sensory signal**, and its routing is **arbitrary**: it takes leg ground-contact events — which in a real fly enter the ventral nerve cord as leg proprioceptive/tactile afferents — and delivers them to the antennal Johnston's-organ population, which has no biological connection to the legs. The routing exists because JON→aDN is the only mechanosensory input→descending pathway for which this project holds verified neuron IDs and a characterised drive response (Part 2b probes), and because the brain-only model omits the nerve cord entirely (assumption register, §7.1), so *any* leg→brain feedback in this model is a construction — this one is at least built from verified parts and says what it is. **Part 2b is evidence that a closed loop can be built and run stably. It is not, and must never be cited as, evidence bearing on the connectome-to-behaviour question** — nor may any Core-B result that uses this channel be described without the label attached. The dust/antennal channel (§3.4) is a different thing — constructed *with* a biological counterpart — and the two labels are not interchangeable.

### 5.4 Walk/halt latency budget

Cadence kept at 100 ms (§2.1, not split). Feedback latency: exactly one chunk (100 ms) from body event to brain drive, plus the decoder filter lag Part 7b specifies for its real decoder (Part 2b's toy used τ = 0.2 s; the figure of merit is the body's own halt timescale, ~0.2 s from command-zero to effective stop per Part 1b — the loop's total sense→stop latency is therefore of order 0.3–0.5 s, and §5.2's halt-latency metric is defined on body state, below, so the accounting stays honest).

### 5.5 Halt event and its measurement (for RQ-C4 / §5.2 metrics)

- **Halt-context event:** the §5.2 food-contact event (front tarsus or labrum vs food geom) turning ON while the fly is walking. In the closed loop this event gates the scenario's sugar stimulus (§3.1, via the §4.5 construction — the same constructed taste signal, on this body's contact signals). Whether the brain then produces the walk-OFF pattern (GABAergic inhibition of walking-promoting DNs, RQ-C4) and the descending signal falls is **the experiment's question, not this spec's promise** — the spec fixes only the event, the gate, and the measurement.
- **The halt source is NOT DECIDED (Amendment Pass 1), stated plainly:** nothing in the current loop design makes the brain stop the walk. The §5.3 JON drive follows leg contact, leg contact follows walking, and walking follows the descending command — a latch, not a halt mechanism: once walking, the feedback sustains the conditions for its own continuation, and no specified brain-side signal turns the descending command off. RQ-C4's walk-OFF pattern is a hypothesis the experiment would test, not a mechanism this spec supplies. **The brain-side halt source is therefore on the §9 not-decided list**, and §5 remains an **interface spec only** until a halt source (a named brain pathway, a named decoder rule, or a named body-side rule — each with its provenance label) is proposed and pre-registered by amendment.
- **Halt latency (body-state measurement):** from the food-contact event's onset chunk (on the §2.3 clock) to the first chunk where thorax forward speed, computed from body positions per chunk, falls below **0.5 mm/s** and stays below for ≥ 2 consecutive chunks **[chosen threshold]** — set against the audit's numbers: walking ≈ 10.7 mm/s; after command-zero, per-0.1 s travel decayed 0.537 → 0.060 → 0.006 mm (≈ 5.4 → 0.6 → 0.06 mm/s), so 0.5 mm/s sits below the first decay bin and marks effective stop, consistent with the audit's "~0.2 s" reading. **Halt-context correctness** (walk-OFF pattern present/absent in brain activity) is read from the brain side per RQ-C4's own preregistration at Part 7b, not defined here.

## 6. The thin FlyBody slice — specified deliverable before the Part 5 loop (Reviewer 1, Part 2b gate)

**Why it exists.** Part 2b proved the bridge on NeuroMechFly walking. The Core-A loop runs on a different body, a different command type (joint targets, not a descending 2-vector) and a different feedback event (labrum contact, not leg transitions). Without a slice, the first time the bridge ever carries the feeding chain would be inside the Part 5 build itself — the exact "bridge last, therefore never" failure this rebuild exists to avoid.

**Minimal form.** Through the **same bridge** (same two processes, same JSON-lines contract, same 100 ms chunks, §2.2 warmup rule, §2.3 settle):

1. A **scripted MN9-rate profile** — pooled rate ramp 0 → 100 → 0 Hz over 6 s (60 chunks), generated in the driver and labelled **synthetic** in every artefact (it is not brain output and impersonates none).
2. The profile passes through the **registered chain itself** — §4.2 estimator + §4.3 mapping, the actual Part 5 code path, not a reimplementation — producing targets for the five proboscis DoFs on the pinned FlyBody in the §4.6 arena.
3. Labrum–food contact is detected per §4.5; the constructed taste event is computed and **written back across the bridge** to the brain-side stub log.
4. Everything is logged per chunk: scripted rate, r_filt, e, the five joint angles, E(t), contact state, taste-event state.

**Success criteria (all required):** (i) 60/60 chunks complete, no worker error, no NaN/Inf in any recorded body state; (ii) E(t) rises while the scripted rate is high and falls as it returns to zero, with onset/offset read by §4.4's definitions (the slice is the first exercise of those definitions); (iii) the labrum–food contact event turns ON during the high-rate phase; (iv) the taste event appears in the brain-side log **exactly one chunk after** the first contact chunk and clears **exactly one chunk after** the contact clears — the §2.1 latency, demonstrated on this chain; (v) zero modifications to the frozen brain code (`fresh/ctb/` untouched — the slice needs no brain at all, which is the point).

**Timing rule for criterion (iv) (Amendment Pass 1).** "First contact chunk" is judged on **chunk-boundary event states**, the same sampling §4.5 defines: the contact chunk is the chunk at whose end the taste-contact event state is first ON. A contact that begins at any point inside a chunk — including its final milliseconds — and leaves the event ON at that chunk's end therefore counts in full to that chunk, and the taste event is due in the brain-side log in the **immediately following chunk, exactly one chunk later**; a contact too brief to survive the §2.4 debounce to the chunk boundary is not a contact event at all, in the slice or anywhere else. Sub-chunk timing can never shift the count by a chunk in either direction, so criterion (iv) needs no mid-chunk placement engineering.

**Failure handling.** A failed criterion is a bridge/chain defect found cheaply: it is fixed at slice level and the slice re-run; **the Part 5 full-loop build does not start until the slice passes.** The slice is a deliverable with its own short record (artefacts + per-criterion verdicts), executed after the qualification gate (§7) — it consumes the gate's pinned body, gate-determined extension signs and Q5-verified arena — and before the Part 5 decoder freeze.

### 6.1 Companion gated deliverables — Part 2b conditions carried forward (Amendment Pass 1)

Three outstanding Part 2b gate conditions stand as deliverables alongside the slice, on the same footing — executed and recorded between the qualification gate and the Part 5 build, each with its own short record:

1. **One long closed episode.** A single closed-loop episode of **10–20 s** (100–200 chunks) on the Part 2b loop configuration, with its success criteria **fixed in writing before it runs**, including: **state-drift checks** (body state and readout statistics in the episode's first and last quarters compared against pre-stated bounds), **memory-growth checks** (worker process memory sampled per chunk; growth beyond a pre-stated bound is a failure, not a curiosity), and **wall-clock reporting** (total and per-chunk wall time recorded — the loop's cost is part of its record, per §2.1's rule that wall-clock is a cost figure). The episode extends Part 2b's 3 × 2 s stability runs; it does not replace them.
2. **The aDN annotation check.** A check of the **v630 annotations** for the descending sets: whether aDN1 / aDN2 have **right-side members** in the annotation that the Part 2 extraction did not return — i.e. whether the left-only aDN2 set is an **extraction omission** or a **true absence** in v630. The answer is recorded either way; if it is an omission, the ID sets are corrected by the Part 2 artefact process (new versioned artefact, never a quiet edit) and §5.1's symmetric-command constraint is revisited by amendment.
3. **The record rules.** Every loop run from here on records: the **frozen ctb commit and tag** it ran against, a **run manifest** (declared drives per §2.2, seeds, configuration), **data hashes**, and a **clean git status at that commit** — a run whose tree was dirty says so in its manifest (as the Part 3 arm's did) rather than tidying the fact away. The record also states the **equivalence limit** of the chunked bridge: Part 2b proved chunked-vs-single-run bit-equivalence for its configuration; that equivalence is claimed **only where it has been probed**, and toggling drives mid-episode is exactly the case the §2.2 harness rule exists to keep out of timed chunks — any configuration that toggles a drive needs its own equivalence probe by the Part 2b method before its chunked results are trusted as single-run results.

### 7.3 Q2 — Range and gain sweep, one joint at a time (also determines the extension signs)

For each of the five proboscis DoFs, in turn: command **9 target positions** evenly spanning its published range via its published actuator (ctrl = target angle; published kp per §4.3), holding each target **0.5 s**, all other proboscis targets held at the settled rest values, neck held, adhesion off. The nine command points are exactly **target_k = range_min + k·(range_max − range_min)/8 for k = 0..8** — endpoints included, no other spacing (Amendment Pass 1). Record measured angle per joint at every physics step.
**Pass, per joint:** (i) **map-relative steady-state error ≤ 0.05 rad at every one of the nine points**, where the terms are defined as: a point's **steady state** is the mean measured angle over the **final 0.1 s** of its 0.5 s hold, and its **steady-state error** is |steady-state mean − q̂_eff(target)|, with q̂_eff the §4.3 clamped static map computed from the compiled artefact's parameters (Amendment Passes 3 and 5). **Before the sweep runs, the predicted steady state for all nine targets on all five joints under the frozen map is computed and recorded in the gate record with a timestamp (Amendment Pass 5) — the sweep tests that recorded prediction, and the 0.05 rad tolerance is unchanged.** (Amendment Pass 3 — Q2 commands the nine raw targets precisely in order to measure the map; raw-target error is not the criterion, because the published servo/spring balance makes raw tracking impossible by construction. What the gate certifies is that the body follows its own published physics, and the measured steady states are recorded per point as the gate's empirical map, which must be **strictly increasing in target wherever the §4.3 clamp does not bind, with plateaus accepted only at clamp-bound points, identified as such in the gate record — a point is clamp-bound if the unclamped q̂ at its target lies outside the published range, identified from the analytic rule, never from the measured plateau**); (ii) no measured angle outside the published range by more than numerical noise (≤ 0.005 rad); (iii) **coupling:** for each non-driven joint, the coupling measure is its **maximum deviation from its rest value across the whole hold**; the measured set is **the other four proboscis DoFs plus every neck joint** (`c_thorax-c_head-pitch`, `c_thorax-c_head-roll`, `c_thorax-c_head-yaw`); pass requires every coupling measure in that set **≤ 0.05 rad**; (iv) no NaN/Inf anywhere.
**Recorded output:** for each pitch joint, the **extension direction** — from the labrum tip's measured displacement (site position) across the sweep: the direction moving the tip away from the head and toward the food plane is extension; §4.4's signs s_j and θ_ext values are fixed from this record and frozen with the gate.

### 7.4 Q3 — Extension, holding, retraction (the full cycle, §0.4(i))

All five DoFs driven together through §4.3's target functions at e following a scripted profile: ramp e 0 → 1 over **0.5 s**, **hold e = 1 for 2.0 s**, ramp back to 0 over 0.5 s, hold 1.0 s. **Five cycles**, adhesion off (joint mechanics isolated from the latch).
**Pass, every cycle:** (i) E(t) (§4.4) reaches **≥ 90% of its commanded value** — the map-predicted achieved E at the §4.3 targets under the §4.3 feedforward (Amendment Pass 3) — by the end of the extension ramp; (ii) during the hold, E stays within **± 0.05 rad** of its hold-phase mean — no creep, no oscillation growth, no instability; (iii) after retraction, E returns to within **0.05 rad** of the pre-cycle reference; (iv) no joint exceeds its published range; no NaN/Inf.

**Q3's claim, stated narrowly (Amendment Pass 3, Reviewer 3's condition):** Q3 qualifies **model-consistent extension only** — that the achieved extension follows the §4.3 map's prediction through a full extend–hold–retract cycle. It does **not** by itself qualify functional feeding reach: a map-predicted extension can be small (the labrum's achieved full-open is ≈0.26 rad), and reaching 90% of a small prediction certifies consistency, not capability. Functional reach is qualified at **Q5** (§7.5): the achieved movement must bring the labrum into contact with the food geom, with contact force reported by the body, inside Q5's single allowed offset adjustment. If it cannot, the gate fails at Q5 under §7.7 — the body is not accepted for the feeding experiment on Q3's evidence alone, and no later document may cite Q3 as evidence of feeding capability.

### 7.5 Q4 — Repeatability (§0.4(ii)) and Q5 — Feeding contact (§0.4(iv))

**Q4.** The Q3 cycle repeated in **5 independent runs** (fresh `Simulation` instances, identical commands, identical pinned artefact). **Pass:** across runs, per-cycle peak E has **range ≤ 0.05 rad**, and event onset times (§4.4) have **range ≤ 100 ms** (one chunk). **Onset resolution (Amendment Pass 1), stated explicitly:** onset times are read by §4.4's definition from chunk-sampled E(t), so their resolution is **one chunk (100 ms)** — the Q4 procedure records per-chunk angles and supports no finer onset claim, and none is made; the ≤ 100 ms range tolerance therefore requires the five runs' onsets to fall in the same or an adjacent chunk. (Finer per-physics-step traces exist in Q2/Q3's records, but redefining onset at step resolution here would silently change §4.4's endpoint; if that is ever wanted, it arrives by amendment to §4.4 first.) MuJoCo stepping is deterministic, so near-identity is expected; the tolerances absorb instance-construction variation, and anything larger is a finding to be explained in the gate record, not averaged away.

**Q5.** The §4.6 arena, fly settled per §2.3, then e ramped to 1 and held. **Pass:** (i) labrum–food contact force > 0 recorded from the body's own contact reporting (`get_bodysegment_contact_forces`), with the §4.5 event turning ON and its timestamps logged; (ii) with §4.3's adhesion rule active, adhesion engages on contact and the contact **persists ≥ 1.0 s** while e = 1 is held (the latch demonstrably holds); (iii) on the retract command, adhesion releases per the rule and the contact event turns OFF; (iv) if the §4.6 starting offset (0.6 mm) makes (i) unreachable, the offset is adjusted once here, the final value recorded, and steps (i)–(iii) re-run at that value (§4.6's construction rule) — **on the same fixed Pass 6 pedestal; the pedestal itself is never adjusted**; (v) **resting-contact check (Amendment Pass 6):** before the ramp, in the settled e = 0 configuration, the labrum–food force must be 0 — contact must be caused by the registered extension, not pre-exist it; a resting contact is a Q5 FAIL. The **predicted contact-onset fraction** is f_c = 0.884, from the §4.6 reach curve, recorded before the re-run; (vi) the **measured contact-onset fraction** (achieved E/E_max at first labrum–food contact) is recorded and reported against 0.884 — a material deviation is a discrepancy for the panel, not a number to be passed over; (vii) **no contact between the pedestal and any body part other than the labrum** may occur during the demo — all pedestal contact pairs are recorded, and any non-labrum pedestal contact is a Q5 FAIL. Tarsal–food contact, if it occurs, is recorded (not required).

### 7.6 Q6 — Brain-to-muscle interface, proven small (§0.4's carried condition; §6.2 bridge tests)

Before any brain is connected to this body: a **synthetic** pooled-rate ladder — constant rates **0, 30, 60, 90, 120 Hz**, each held 1.5 s, labelled synthetic in every artefact — through §4.2's estimator and §4.3's mapping, driving **rostrum pitch only** (other targets at rest). The mapping needs an r_lo/r_hi pair before Part 5 calibration exists, so Q6 uses **r_lo = 5 Hz (the FIXED §4.3 value — not provisional)** and a **provisional r_hi = 155 Hz** — the pooled MN9 rate the validated model actually produced in Part 3 (92.57 + 62.40 Hz) — **labelled provisional in every gate artefact and replaced by the calibrated r_hi at the Part 5 freeze** (Amendment Pass 1). Q6's pass criteria (zero point, monotonicity) do not depend on the exact r_hi, which is why a provisional value is legitimate here and nowhere else. **Pass:** (i) at 0 Hz the rostrum target and measured angle stay at rest (zero-in/zero-out); (ii) measured E_rostrum (rostrum term of §4.4) is **monotone non-decreasing** across the ladder, with plateaus acceptable only where the §4.3 clamp binds, identified as such in the record; (iii) the rate→angle calibration curve is saved as a gate artefact. If this small case does not hold, the gate stops here and reports the negative result — the full embodiment is not attempted on an unproven interface.

### 7.7 Gate verdict rule

Q0–Q6 each record PASS/FAIL with their numbers. **The gate passes only if every step passes.** The gate also fails if the §4.4 band guard fails (3 × ε_res ≥ 0.10 rad), because θ_off then cannot be frozen below the onset threshold. Reruns of a failed step are permitted within the time-box only for gate-*script* defects (a bug in my measurement code, shown to be one); a failure of the body, the actuators, or the interface is a gate failure, full stop, and §7's no-quiet-fallback rule fires. The gate's acceptance of the body for the feeding experiment rests on **Q5's contact demonstration** for functional reach; Q3 certifies model-consistent extension only (Amendment Pass 3).
