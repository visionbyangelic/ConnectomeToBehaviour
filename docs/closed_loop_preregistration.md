## 1. Scope — what this freeze covers, and what each run set certifies

This preregistration covers two run sets under **one** Core-A freeze — one decoder, one arena, one chain, one seed policy:

- **Part 5 characterisation runs (§5 of this document):** the closed feeding loop, run as built, in four arms — closed-loop sugar (event-gated), open-loop sugar (scripted window), no-drive control, and sugar+bitter — **plus, as amended (sensitivity arms, 24 Nov 2026), a fifth arm (e): closed-loop bilateral sugar, registered as a separate secondary stimulus condition**, the embodied counterpart of the rung's condition (v). These runs answer: does the assembled loop behave as the registered chain predicts, does closing the loop change the outcome against open-loop replay of the same stimulus definition (RQ-P3, feeding instance — required inside Core-A by plan §3.1 item 5), and does bitter suppress the sugar response through the brain (RQ-C2, embodied instance)? Arm (e) answers none of those questions; it is descriptive (§5).
- **Part 7 intervention arms (§8 of this document):** the feeding intervention experiment's lesion family — targeted block-output lesions, matched control block-output lesions, each under the frozen decoder and under a refit decoder — estimating **E-Pathway** for feeding (plan §2.1, §5.1).

**What a Part 5 pass certifies, and what it does not.** The characterisation runs certify the *loop as built*: that the frozen brain, the registered chain and the qualified body, assembled, produce the feeding behaviour the chain predicts, with the loop's feedback genuinely closing (contact sustaining drive). They do **not** certify that the connectome's topology causes the behaviour — that is the intervention experiment's question, and no characterisation result, however clean, may be reported as topology evidence. Conversely, a characterisation failure (the loop does not extend, does not contact, does not sustain) is a finding about the assembled system — decoder, threshold character, arena — diagnosed under §13; it is not evidence about the connectome either, and it does not by itself implicate K1 (the brain was validated at Part 3 on the sugarR rung) or license any parameter change outside the amendment rule.

**What the Part 7 arms certify, and what they do not.** The lesion family estimates E-Pathway *for the two targeted sets named in §8*, through this decoder and this body, under the claim scoping of §10. It does not estimate E-Total (intact vs null families) or E-Baseline (intact vs rule/body-only baselines): those estimands' intact side is frozen by this document (arm (a) and the Part 7 intact configuration), but their comparison sides — the D1–D4 shuffle networks (plan §5.3) and the C1–C3 rule baselines (plan §5.5) — **do not exist yet as artefacts**, and their construction (network generation procedure; baseline construction and optimisation budgets) is itself pre-registrable work. **Registered scope decision (ruled on at Amendment Pass 1):** this freeze does not silently absorb them. **E-Total and E-Baseline are not registered outcomes of this freeze.** E-Total and E-Baseline arms may run only under follow-on preregistration amendments to this document that register the construction procedures — procedures **justified on their own terms, not by reference to the already-known intact results** — and each such amendment must be **panel-approved and frozen before the Part 5/Part 7 data are examined for those estimands and before any network or baseline is built**. The body-only/decoder-only baselines of plan §5.3 (brain output replaced by zeros / constant rates) are partially covered here: arm (c) (§5) is the no-drive control, and the chain's zero-in/zero-out property is a premise check (§3), not an experimental arm.

## 2. The system under test — the loop as built

One episode is one embodied run of the following assembly. Every element is fixed by a governing document; this section assembles, it does not redesign.

- **Brain.** The frozen implementation (header), driven per the Part 4 §3 stimulus specs: Poisson drive at 200 Hz per driven neuron while a drive is ON (sugar/bitter/water sets from `id_sets_v1`: sugar right 21, sugar left 10, bitter 21, water 18; the driven set is declared per scenario in §5/§8 and in every run manifest). Block-output operations (Part 7) use the frozen model's `block_output()` — outgoing weights of the named neurons zeroed — with the Part 2 wording discipline: the operation blocks a population's *output*; it is not a deletion and not a silencing of its inputs.
- **Bridge.** The two-process bridge of Part 2b/Part 4 §2.1: brain and body advance in 100 ms chunks; the command driving body chunk *k* is decoded from brain chunk *k*, whose sensory state was set by body chunk *k−1* — exactly one chunk (100 ms) of simulated feedback latency. The harness rule (Part 4 §2.2) binds every run: every population any part of the run can drive is declared in the manifest before the episode clock starts, and the brain worker pays all compile costs in a zero-duration warmup with all declared drives active. **A drive that is not in the manifest may not be switched on mid-run.**
- **Settle.** Part 4 §2.3, unchanged: zero-command warmup chunks until three consecutive chunks each show ≤ 2 leg-contact transitions and ≤ 0.01 mm thorax-height change; cap 30 chunks; an episode that does not settle is recorded INVALID in its manifest, counts as a failed episode, and is never replaced, extended or re-run under the same episode ID. t = 0 is the first chunk boundary after settle; feedback is armed only from t = 0. Settle uses no brain input of any kind, so invalidity cannot depend on the arm, condition or lesion state. Every run report states its invalid-episode count and rate.
- **Decoder chain.** `repo/src/closed_loop/chain.py` implementing Part 4 §4.2 + §4.3 exactly: MN9 L/R spike counts → trailing 500 ms boxcar per side (startup rule: chunks 0–4 divide by elapsed time) → pooled rate r = r_L + r_R → EMA τ = 0.2 s (α = 0.3935 per chunk, initial state 0) → r_filt → e = clip((r_filt − r_lo)/(r_hi − r_lo), 0, 1) with r_lo = 5 Hz FIXED and r_hi from §4 of this document → per-joint intended achieved angles interpolating θ_rest → θ_ext (gate outputs: signs s = −1 for both pitch joints; θ_ext rostrum −0.9538 rad, haustellum pitch −1.2231 rad, labrum L/R +0.2625 rad) → feedforward inverse through the clamped static map computed from the compiled artefact's parameters (kp 10 head / 1 labrum, ks 3, ref 0 — the analytic rule is the frozen object; the gate's empirical map is its audit) → ctrl written via `set_actuator_inputs`, zero-order hold per chunk. At e = 0 the chain writes ctrl = 0 to the five proboscis DoFs. Haustellum yaw and all three neck joints are held at their settled values for the whole episode, **in every arm**. Labrum adhesion per Part 4 §4.3: engages in the first chunk after the taste-contact event turns ON; releases after the event has been OFF for 2 consecutive chunks.
- **Body and arena.** The pinned FlyBody artefact (hashes in §3) in the qualified arena (Part 4 §4.6 as revised): food disc (cylinder r 0.5 mm, h 0.05 mm) on the fixed pedestal (r 0.5 mm, h 0.28 mm), disc top at ground + 0.33 mm, centre 0.6 mm in front of the labrum tip's settled ground projection (the gate's final offset; the §7.5(iv) adjustment was not used). **The arena construction includes MJCF contact pairs for the six proboscis geoms against both arena geoms** — the qualification gate's execution-#5 defect fix; without them the §4.5 event cannot exist (§3 premise check P2). Upstream labels FlyBody support in FlyGym "experimental"; that word travels with the body wherever it is described.
- **Feedback.** The constructed taste event (Part 4 §4.5): ON when either labrum segment or any front tarsus segment sustains contact force > 0 against the food geom for ≥ 10 consecutive physics steps; OFF after ≥ 50 consecutive steps with no qualifying contact (Part 4 §2.4 debounce). Event state is sampled at chunk boundaries: **the event state at the end of body chunk *k* gates the scenario's sugar drive in brain chunk *k+1*.** While the event is ON, the scenario's declared sugar stimulus is driven; while OFF, the sugar drive is off — unless the scenario separately declares a scripted stimulus window, in which case both gates are logged per chunk and the scenario says which is which (§5's arms depend on this distinction).
- **Provenance, in the required words.** The contact is **physical contact**; the signal it produces is a **constructed sensory signal**; it is **not biological gustatory transduction**, and no document, figure or talk from this project may call it taste *sensation* without the word "constructed" attached. The join (contact → GRN drive) is encoder, class **D**; the GRN→MN9 dynamics it gates are **E1**; the estimator and mapping are class **D**; the body's movement is the published model's physics. The full chain table is Part 4 §4.7 and is incorporated by reference. Feeding claims from this freeze are **E1 + D** (plan §3.4); they become E2 only to the extent the Part 7 separation results support it, under §10's permitted wording.

## 4. Calibration — the one fitted constant, under the binding separation rule

Part 4 §4.3 fixes the rule; this section fixes only what §4.3 leaves to Part 5: the calibration set's size, shape and seeds, and the exact sampling definition of the percentile. **The rule, restated so this document stands alone:** every episode is designated **calibration** or **evaluation** in writing here, before either kind runs; **only designated calibration sugar-only episodes determine r_hi**; evaluation episodes never contribute to fitting or revising r_hi, in whole or in part, under any re-analysis; r_hi is computed **once**, recorded and **hashed before any evaluation episode runs**, and the hash travels with the frozen decoder; if r_hi ≤ r_lo + 10 Hz = 15 Hz, the decoder is **rejected** — no rescue by changing the fitting rule, the calibration dataset, or the split; a rebuilt decoder starts again under the same rule.

**Calibration set (fixed at Amendment Pass 1 on the panel's ruling).** **10 episodes**, each the standard episode shape (§2), configuration: intact brain, **sugar-only, scripted-window** — the right sugar GRN set (21 neurons) driven at 200 Hz for the full stimulus period (chunks 0–59) regardless of contact, taste gate logged but not gating. Seeds **5000–5009**. Reasoning: (i) scripted, not gated — calibration measures the brain-plus-estimator response to sustained sugar drive; gating it on contact would couple the fitted constant to contact success, the circularity §7.3 exists to prevent; (ii) the window: the estimator reaches steady state by chunk 20 (§2), so each episode contributes its chunks 20–59 — 40 samples; (iii) the count: the percentile (§4.3's rule) is taken over the pooled per-chunk sample defined below — 10 episodes × 40 chunks = **400 samples**, at which a 95th percentile is a stable order statistic; Part 3 measured the underlying variability as small (per-trial MN9 SD ≈ 3.5 Hz on a ≈ 93 Hz mean, from the Part 3 CI widths at N = 30), so 10 independent episodes amply determine the pooled distribution the constant is read from. Calibration episodes are **not** evaluation data: they appear in no endpoint, comparison or figure of §5/§8.

**The percentile, defined exactly (the specs name the statistic but not its sampling unit — this definition was PROPOSED precision in the draft and is fixed at Amendment Pass 1 on the panel's ruling).** For each calibration episode, take r_filt (§4.2's filtered pooled rate, as the chain computes it chunk by chunk) at chunks 20–59 inclusive. Pool those values across all 10 episodes (400 values). **r_hi is the 95th percentile of that pooled sample** (linear interpolation between closest ranks, the numpy default — the implementation is `chain.py`'s companion calibration script, hashed at the freeze). Per-chunk values within an episode are autocorrelated by construction (boxcar + EMA); that is acceptable here because the percentile is a *fitting statistic for a decoder constant*, not an inferential estimate — no CI is claimed for r_hi, and no conclusion in §5/§8 treats it as one. Each episode's own steady-window mean r_filt is reported alongside, so the pooled distribution's episode-level spread is on record.

**Expected value, stated in advance so the freeze can be checked against it.** Part 3's validated pooled response to this exact drive is 92.57 + 62.40 = **154.97 Hz** (brain-only, 1 s trials). In the loop, r_filt at steady drive fluctuates around the pooled rate with the estimator's smoothing; the 95th percentile of the steady sample is therefore expected **above** ≈ 155 Hz — how far above is what calibration measures. The completed §6 E(r) table is computed from the measured r_hi at the freeze and hashed with the decoder (Part 4 §4.4).

**The threshold character, confronted (Part 4 §9, carried here as required).** Contact begins only at achieved E/E_max ≈ **0.837** (the measured direct-protocol onset, §10 of this document) — the constructed taste feedback is a **threshold**, not a graded signal. At steady state E = e · E_max (Part 4 §4.4), so sustained contact in the gated arm requires sustained **e ≥ 0.837**, i.e. sustained r_filt ≥ r_lo + 0.837 × (r_hi − r_lo). With r_hi at its 95th-percentile definition, the *mean* steady r_filt (≈ 155 Hz by Part 3) sits **below** r_hi by construction, so the calibrated decoder's steady-state e at the mean sugar response is **below 1** — expected ≈ (155 − 5)/(r_hi − 5) — and whether it clears 0.837 depends on the measured r_hi: it clears the threshold if and only if r_hi ≲ 184 Hz. **Pre-registered consequences, so none of this is negotiated after the fact:** (i) calibration reports, alongside r_hi, the implied steady-state e at the calibration episodes' mean steady r_filt, and the implied contact margin e_steady − 0.837; (ii) if e_steady < 0.837, the prediction for arm (a) is that contact is intermittent or absent — driven by r_filt fluctuations crossing the threshold, not by sustained extension — and arm (a)'s contact endpoints are then interpreted under that prediction (duty cycle becomes the informative endpoint; a zero-contact arm (a) is a result about the decoder/threshold system, reported as such, **not** a licence to lower r_hi, raise the drive, or move the pedestal — every one of those is an amendment that voids the affected arms' confirmatory status and must say so); (iii) the same arithmetic applies a fortiori to any condition that lowers the pooled rate — in particular sugar+bitter (§5, arm (d)): if suppression brings r_filt below the contact threshold, the taste feedback switches off entirely in gated configurations, and claims for such conditions are scoped to the neural endpoints, per §11.

**The two r_hi numbers in this section, derived — they guard opposite tails (Amendment Pass 1 — Reviewer 1, items 4 and 6).** r_hi is a percentile of the pooled filtered rate, in Hz; the two bounds that involve it are not in tension, because they guard opposite tails of its possible values. The **rejection bound (r_hi ≤ r_lo + 10 Hz = 15 Hz) guards the low tail**: a decoder whose 95th-percentile steady filtered rate barely exceeds r_lo = 5 Hz has no dynamic range — its e would sit at ≈ 0 under the very drive it is meant to decode. That rejection is Part 4 §4.3's rule, and its consequence for this freeze is stated here so it cannot be negotiated later: **no evaluation episode runs under a rejected decoder; the freeze's decoder item is void** (this document's other frozen items — arena, seeds, arms, analysis — stand); a rebuilt decoder is a new design under the same separation rule, arriving by amendment with the rejection attached (§13 item 2). The **≈ 184 Hz figure guards the high side and is not a rejection**: it is the threshold-clearance condition, derived from e_steady = (r̄ − r_lo)/(r_hi − r_lo) ≥ 0.837 with r̄ = 154.97 Hz, Part 3's measured steady pooled rate under this exact drive (§4's expected value, above). The 0.837 here is the **measured direct-protocol contact onset — execution #5, Q5(vi), measured onset fraction 0.8371 (§10)** — as distinct from the reach curve's quasi-static prediction f_c = 0.884 (Part 4 Pass 6), which is the prediction the measured value was compared against; §10 keeps both numbers and the signed residual visible. If the calibrated r_hi lands above ≈ 184 Hz, the prediction for arm (a) is intermittent or absent contact, under the consequences (ii) above — a registered finding under §11 — and the decoder is **retained**: a high r_hi compresses e toward the threshold; it does not void the decoder.

## 6. Endpoints and measurement definitions

All body-side measurements use Part 4 §4.4's definitions verbatim: E(t) = s_ros·(θ_ros(t) − θ_rest,ros) + s_haus·(θ_haus(t) − θ_rest,haus) from the episode's own settled rest pose; **onset** = first chunk with E ≥ 0.10 rad sustained ≥ 2 consecutive chunks; **offset** = first later chunk with E < θ_off = **0.01036 rad** (frozen, §3) sustained ≥ 2 consecutive chunks; duration = offset − onset; an excursion never sustaining 2 chunks above 0.10 rad is a sub-threshold excursion — logged, never promoted, never silently dropped.

**Graded primary endpoints** (per episode, then across seeds per §7):
1. **Peak E** (rad) over the episode.
2. **Mean E** (rad) over the stimulus period (chunks 0–59).
3. **MN9 firing rates, L and R separately** (Hz): mean per-side rate over the stimulus period, from the brain's spike monitor. **Never pooled in any endpoint or figure**, with the single registered exception of endpoint 4's suppression ratio, which is defined on the pooled neural readout (endpoint 4) with L and R ratios reported alongside; the firing-rate endpoints themselves are always per-side (RQ-C5 discipline, §1); otherwise the pooled rate appears only as the chain's internal variable and in calibration reporting.
4. **Bitter-suppression ratio** (arm (d) vs arm (b), paired by seed): per seed, (pooled MN9 rate in (d) over chunks 0–59) ÷ (pooled MN9 rate in (b) over chunks 0–59) — the ratio is computed on the pooled *neural* readout for this endpoint only, because §5.2's suppression metric is a neural metric; L and R ratios are reported alongside. (Part 0 §2.1's secondary feeding metric; TOST bounds ±0.15, §7.)

**Thresholded secondary endpoints** (per episode):
5. **Extension onset latency** (chunks from t = 0 to §4.4 onset; episodes with no onset are recorded as no-onset, not censored silently — the no-onset count per arm is a reported number).
6. **Contact onset fraction**: achieved E/E_max at the first chunk whose end-state has the taste event ON — measured on the achieved-E axis, against the §10 materiality band around 0.8371.
7. **Contact duration**: taste-event ON time within the episode (total ON chunks), and the longest continuous ON stretch; **taste-event duty cycle** = ON chunks ÷ chunks in the gate-armed period (arm (a): chunks 20–99; arms (b)/(d): chunks 0–99, reported descriptively since the event does not gate there).
8. **Ignition outcome** (arm (a) only): whether contact occurred at all, and whether drive persisted via the gate beyond the ignition window (any gate-ON chunk in 20–59).

**The registered rate→extension prediction.** Part 4 §4.4's table, completed at the freeze with the measured E_max (2.1746 rad, §3) and the calibrated r_hi (§4): at steady state E(r) = e(r) · E_max with e(r) = clip((r − 5)/(r_hi − 5), 0, 1), at pooled rates r ∈ {0, 25, 50, 75, 100, 125, 155} Hz. The completed table is **hashed with the decoder** at the freeze. **Nonlinearity, acknowledged as required:** the table is a *steady-state* prediction of the registered ramp; the Q6 ladder already shows the measured chain departing from a naive linear reading — its hold-mean rostrum extensions (0.1498 / 0.3742 / 0.5295 / 0.8066 rad at 30 / 60 / 90 / 120 Hz, under the provisional r_hi = 155) sit up to ≈ 0.08 rad from the table's rostrum-term values at the same rates, because Q6's numbers are dynamic hold-means of a realising estimator, not steady states. No analysis under this freeze may assume a linear rate→extension relation anywhere the table does not state one, and measured extension curves are reported **against** the table with the §10 band — a systematic departure is a finding about the mapping or the body, not a licence to refit the table (Part 4 §4.4).

**Endpoint hierarchy for the conflict analysis** (Part 4 §4.4, Amendment Pass 1 — binding): for the sugar+bitter family, the **graded endpoints (peak E, mean E, MN9 rates, suppression ratio) are primary**; the thresholded endpoints are secondary. A conflict claim made on a thresholded endpoint alone, against the graded endpoints, is reported as threshold-conditional, in those words.

### 8.1 Matched-control selection — procedure fixed here, before any intervention outcome exists

Plan §5.3 (Reviewer 3's adopted amendment) requires matching on more than count, the procedure specified in the preregistration before outcomes are observed, and remaining imbalance quantified and reported. **Procedure (fixed at Amendment Pass 1 on the panel's ruling):**

- **Candidate pool:** all v630 neurons **except** — the targeted sets; every driven population (sugar L/R, bitter, water, Ir94e, JON-CE/F/D_m/all); MN9; the three-inhibitory set; aDN1, aDN2-left, aBN1. No control neuron may belong to any named functional set of this project.
- **Features** (from frozen artefacts only): in-degree, out-degree, in/out synapse totals (Part 1 connectivity), predicted transmitter sign (the frozen loader's sign assignment), baseline mean firing rate (the Part 3 primary run's per-neuron rates — a frozen output).
- **Generation:** for targeted set T, control sets are built one-to-one: each member of T is matched to a distinct pool neuron by nearest distance in the standardised feature space (each feature normalised by its pooled SD), without replacement within or across the five sets of a lesion family, under selection seed **5700** with sub-seeds 5701, 5702, … drawn in order for successive candidate sets. Candidate sets are generated in sub-seed order; within a draw, sets are considered in generation order and the first five passing all tolerances are taken; any exact distance tie in one-to-one matching is broken by the lower neuron ID — the procedure is deterministic given the seed and outcome-blind, since no feature derives from any intervention outcome. **Acceptance tolerances (fixed at Amendment Pass 1 on the panel's ruling):** set size exactly |T|; mean in-degree and mean out-degree within ±25% of T's; excitatory fraction within ±0.10 of T's; mean baseline rate within ±25% of T's or ±5 Hz, whichever is wider (the Part 0 band). The first five candidate sets per lesion passing all tolerances are the controls. If the screen cannot be met within 100 seeded draws for a lesion, the five lowest-distance sets are used and the screen's failure is reported as part of the imbalance record — the shortfall is never hidden by widening a tolerance after the fact.
- **Freeze of the sets:** all ten sets' IDs, their feature table, and the achieved imbalance per §5.3 criterion are frozen as an artefact (CSV + JSON in `repo/src/closed_loop/`) **before any Part 7 episode runs**, and its hash joins the freeze record (§14).
- **Known matching limitation, stated in advance:** plan §5.3 items (4) neuropil/anatomical location and (5) downstream connectivity/network influence are matched only as far as project artefacts allow. The v630 artefacts in this project carry **no annotation table** (Part 2b record), so neuropil matching is **not feasible** and is registered as unmatched; downstream influence is proxied by out-synapse totals and out-degree, which are in the feature set. Matching therefore covers §5.3 items (1) degree distributions, (2) baseline activity, (3) sign composition and (6) perturbation magnitude (via synapse totals), proxies (5), and does not cover (4). The E-Pathway interpretation is restricted accordingly (§11): a lesion result is interpretable against its controls *on the matched features*, and the unmatched anatomical dimension is named wherever the result is reported.

### 8.2 Decoder variants — frozen (primary) and refit (secondary), the refit rule stated exactly

Plan §5.4: primary analysis under the **frozen** decoder; secondary under a decoder **refit per condition** with an identical, pre-declared fitting budget; both reported, neither hidden; if refitting erases a separation, that is the finding.

- **Frozen variant:** every configuration runs with §4's r_hi — the intact calibration — hashed at the freeze. Nothing else differs between configurations: same chain, same r_lo, same body, same arena (plan §0.4's identical-body rule, applied to the whole stack).
- **Refit variant — the rule, exactly:** for configuration X, r_hi^X is computed by **§4's procedure, unchanged**, applied to X's **calibration counterpart**: 10 episodes identical to §4's calibration set in shape, sampling (chunks 20–59), pooling and percentile, except that the brain carries X's block-output from before t = 0. Seeds from the §7 allocation (blocks of 10 from 5400, in §8's table order). r_hi^X is computed once and hashed before X's refit evaluation episodes run. The fitting budget is therefore identical for every configuration by construction — §4's procedure is the budget. Only r_hi differs between the frozen and refit variants of a configuration; every other chain element is bit-identical.
- **The degenerate cases are decided here, not at run time:** if r_hi^X ≤ r_lo + 10 Hz = 15 Hz, §4.3's rejection rule fires for X's refit decoder: **the rejection is the recorded refit result for X**, no refit evaluation episodes run for X, and the frozen/refit gap for X is reported in the words of §11's table. This is the *expected* outcome for **L-MN9** — its estimator input is identically zero under the lesion, so its calibration counterpart yields r_hi^X = 0 by construction — and a likely outcome for **L-GRN**. Registering this in advance is the point: a refit that "rescues" a lesioned readout by construction would be a decoder artefact, and the registered rule refuses it in both directions. The rejection triggers only on the low tail (r_hi^X ≤ 15 Hz); a high r_hi^X does not reject — it compresses e toward the threshold arithmetic of §4, which is reported, not repaired.
- **Intact:** the intact configuration's refit is §4's calibration itself (r_hi^INT = r_hi); it is not re-run and is never counted as an independent refit.

## 9. Pre-run deliverable — the sugar-drive toggle equivalence probe (§6.1(3)) — EXECUTED, as amended at Pass 1

Part 2b proved chunked-vs-single-run bit-equivalence for its configuration, and Part 4 §6.1(3) states the limit: **that equivalence is claimed only where it has been probed, and toggling drives mid-episode is exactly the case that needs its own probe.** Every arm in this document toggles the sugar drive mid-episode (scripted windows at chunks 0/60; the taste gate at body-determined chunks). The gated arms' chunked results are therefore not trusted until this probe is discharged.

**The drafted design was impossible in this stack — a premise correction, recorded before any evaluation data exist.** The draft's probe compared the bridge against a single continuous run with the same toggle schedule applied *internally* at the same chunk boundaries. The Part 5 build attempted both constructions of such a reference and neither exists (record: `closed_loop_build.md` §3): Brian2 fixes a run's active set at run start, so toggling the drives' `active` flags from inside one run (a network_operation applying the schedule) produced **zero spikes** — a drive inactive at run start is absent from the schedule and a mid-run flip can never reach it (attempt preserved in `probe_toggle_equivalence_attempt1.json`); and modulating the drives' rate instead is impossible, because Brian2 2.9's `PoissonInput.rate` is a read-only property. The frozen `Brain.run()` has no scheduling surface either. Amendment Pass 1 therefore replaces the drafted design with the probe as executed by the build — the Part 2b method, which §6.1(3) itself names: bit-identical chunking under a constant drive, plus exact toggling between runs. Any stronger claim would require a different drive mechanism, which would be a different model.

**The executed probe** (configuration fixed before running: sugar right set at 200 Hz, seed **5103**, 20 chunks × 100 ms; every run in a **fresh process, the network built exactly once, at build position #1** — the Part 2b record-completion qualification (§10.1 of `first_loop_build.md`): successive same-seed builds *within one process* alternate between two RNG streams, so a spike train depends on build position as well as seed, and comparisons across build positions are invalid by construction; canonicalisation and SHA-256 exactly as Part 2b's hash re-check; trains archived in `repo/results/closed_loop/probe_toggle/`):

- **(i) Constant-drive chunked-vs-single bit-identity.** Chunked (20 × 100 ms, persistent Network, the worker's §2.2 warmup) vs single (one 2000 ms run), drive on throughout: **bit-identical — 34,013 spikes each, SHA-256 `7675ebe18c3775ad48542b62f7557a0331ad3023d2291da2f1284469fa0fe5d1` both.** Chunking is neutral at this duration, seed and warmup.
- **(ii) Toggle exactness — run-to-run reproducibility of the chunked toggle train.** Toggle schedule OFF at chunks 0–4, ON at 5–14, OFF at 15–19, applied by the worker at chunk boundaries: the driven population's count is **exactly 0 in every OFF chunk** and **383–439 in every ON chunk**; the chunked toggle train is 17,379 spikes, SHA-256 `0ead53e3d83b163a610de1701f5a390503822d271db5ff9526355437e9778387`. ## 13. On failure — diagnosis paths, in pre-set order

Per the plan's discrepancy rule: STOP, log, diagnose — in the order below, each step's outcome recorded before the next begins. **No parameter is re-fit, no threshold moved, no arena element adjusted during diagnosis.** Any proposal to change a registered quantity is a formal amendment that voids the affected runs' confirmatory status and must say so.

1. **Premise failures (P1–P6).** A failed premise check stops its run set before any scientific criterion is evaluated. A *harness/script defect, shown to be one* (as the gate's pair-coverage defect was shown: geometric penetration with zero generated contacts), may be fixed and the check re-run, with both artefacts preserved and the defect recorded in full. A failure of the body, the chain's mathematics, or the determinism property is not a script defect: it returns to the panel by formal amendment.
2. **Calibration rejection (r_hi ≤ 15 Hz).** The decoder is rejected under §4.3's rule. Path: the rejection is recorded with the calibration data; a rebuilt decoder is a new design under the same separation rule, arriving by amendment to this document with the rejection attached. The calibration episodes are never re-picked, re-windowed or re-pooled to rescue the constant.
3. **Characterisation failure** (the loop does not extend, contact or sustain as §10 predicts). Order: (i) the chain code against its unit tests and the P4 audit; (ii) the arena — pair coverage (P2) re-verified on the exact build that ran, placement re-measured; (iii) the estimator's realised values against M5 (a convergence failure is an implementation finding); (iv) the §4 threshold arithmetic — e_steady against 0.837 — computed from the run's own r_filt record: if the system behaved as the arithmetic predicts, the outcome is the §11 "did not sustain" finding, **not** a defect, and the path forward (accept the scoped claim, or amend the design with confirmatory status voided) is Angelic's decision at the gate, with the panel; (v) only then, system-level explanations (body dynamics under sustained drive, adhesion behaviour over long holds), each with evidence.
4. **Settle-failure pattern.** Invalid episodes cluster → the spawn/arena construction is diagnosed (Part 4 §2.3: a pattern is a finding about the spawn specification, fixed by amendment to the spawn specification — the settle definition itself is never relaxed mid-run).
5. **Analysis disputes.** The analysis script is re-verified on synthetic episode records with known endpoint values before any real output is re-examined (Part 3 §9 step 1 discipline: the measuring instrument is the cheapest explanation and is excluded first).
